- Start with Adam’s question from last week: is God commanding “no other gods before me” because the gods of the nations are real?

**Exodus 20:3 > no other gods**

- NO OTHER GODS: so what does this mean? Why doesn’t it say “there are no other gods”? Why does it say “you shall have no other gods besides me” as if there are other gods?
  - MODERN UNDERSTANDING: The modern understanding of this is that God is implying that all other gods are false, they are not real. But is that what it says? Has there been anywhere in the 41 classes we’ve had before this **where the text ever gave any indication that the other gods were not real?** So why are we reading that into the text when it isn’t there?
    - WRITE CHAPTERS ON BOARD: Recall from **Genesis 6**, the “sons of God” transgressed the boundary between the spiritual realm and the physical realm. We learned in **Job 38** that the “sons of God” were around before the earth was created, when God determined the measurements of the earth and laid its cornerstone because they “shouted for joy”. We also learned that these “sons of God” appear again in **Deuteronomy 32** when Moses is retelling the Tower of Babel incident and he says that the nations were created “according to the number of the sons of God”: i.e. every son got a nation as their inheritance from their father, but God would act as his own son and keep one nation as his own inheritance, the nation of Israel. We then saw in **Psalm 82** that God (Elohim) sits among the gods (elohim) and holds judgment: to his sons who were the gods of the other nations he accuses them of corruption, “I said, “**You are gods, sons of the Most High, all of you; nevertheless, like men you shall die,and fall like any prince.**”
    - SONG OF MOSES: remember from the Song of Moses in Exodus 15 the phrase “**Who is like you, O Yahweh, among the gods?**” And I pointed out at the time, if what Moses is saying here is “other gods do not exist”, then what praise is it to say “**Who is like you, O Yahweh, among things that don’t exist? You exist and they don’t!**” But you could say the same thing about me since I also exist and the gods don’t “**Who is like you, O Aaron, among the gods?**” But that’s not exactly high praise worthy of the Song of Moses. No, it’s pretty clear Moses meant “**among the most powerful beings we know of, you are in a class by yourself, Yahweh**”.
    - GODS AS DEMONS: The gods of the nations exist, and they are corrupt, and Yahweh’s inheritance, his people, are not to worship them. It even tells us later in **Deuteronomy 32** that “**Jeshurun (Israel) grew fat and kicked**” and **worshipped “new Elohim that had come recently” who were demons**. Jeremiah complains about these “demons” who are not Yahweh in **Jeremiah 2:10**: “For cross to the coasts of Cyprus and see, or send to Kedar and examine with care; see if there has been such a thing. **Has a nation changed its Elohim**, even though they are no Elohim? But my people have changed their glory for that which does not profit.” Yahweh says **the other nations seem to have no trouble worshipping their demon gods** (the “new \[demon\] Elohim who had come recently” from Deuteronomy 32), **but Israel constantly strays.**
    - PAUL SETTLES THE QUESTION: 1 Corinthians 10:20: “What do I imply then? That food offered to idols is anything, or that an idol is anything? No, I imply that what pagans sacrifice they offer to demons and not to God. I do not want you to be participants with demons.” So the original “gods” of the nations were the sons of Yahweh, but “new gods” arose later who were demons and people have been worshipping those ever since.
  - MODERN GODS: But this is one of the 10 Commandments, so it must be important, and “don’t believe in things that don’t exist” just doesn’t seem important enough. So the typical Christian sermon on this commandment is to **redefine “other gods” to mean anything in your life that is more important than Yahweh**. Money, sex, drugs, prestige, sports, your job, etc. It’s not bad advice and certainly aligns with the rest of the Biblical narrative. Do not worship money or sex or drugs or fame over Yahweh. Yahweh we should be first in your life.
  - IS THAT WHAT MOSES WOULD HAVE SAID? But now imagine, do you think Moses came back down the mountain and said “**Ok guys, I know Yahweh told you not to have any other gods besides him, but what he really meant was all other gods are not real and you should not worship money and sex instead of him.**” That sort of **self-focused, modern therapeutic language just seems out of place** here at Sinai. So it is true we should not do those things, but it is also true that’s not what this commandment says.
  - ELOHIM: remember, we use the word “gods”, but the Hebrew word is Elohim. It’s used of many things in the Bible: Yahweh himself as “the” most high Elohim, of the sons of Yahweh, of angels and demons, and of the dead spirit of Samuel the prophet who the witch at Endor conjures up for Saul.   **It seems to mean “spirit being”. It does not mean “money, sex, drugs, fame.”**
  - TRIBAL GOD AS INTRODUCTION: Yahweh’s first Commandment is an identification of who he is, and that he used to be worshiped by his people to the exclusion of all other gods. Notice he introduced himself to Israel as their tribal God. **He is not commanding that the other nations must worship him. His commanding that his people must worship him.** As we discussed back in Genesis 6, Deuteronomy 32 will show us that the spiritual beings which God calls his sons were allocated to each of the nations as their gods. **What this commandment actually says is if you are a member of the nation of Israel (child of the promise), you shall have no other Elohim before Yahweh the Elohim who took you out of Egypt.**
- THIS IS THE GOD WE FOLLOW: who takes us out of slavery and cares enough about us to not be willing to share us with other gods. Thank you Yahweh for commanding us to have no other gods.

**Exodus 20:4-6 > 2: no images or idols**

- HOW NOT TO WORSHIP YAHWEH: so first he tells them I am your God you will have none other, and now he says, “**do not worship me like the other gods.**” **Do not make idols or things that you can worship in space.** As Jesus said And John chapter 4 to the woman at the well **God is spirit, and you must worship him in spirit.** He is not in our physical world. **Except the one time he was, in the person of his son.**  So you don’t worship him like you worship all the other gods, by making idols and praying to them, hoping that the elohim will inhabit the thing you made.
- JEWS AREN’T ARTISTS: historically, it is because of this commandment that you don’t find a lot of Jewish artists or painters anywhere. So why is one of the Ten Commandments that you can’t make images or carvings?   **The consensus among the commentators is because when you make images, they inevitably become worshipped.**  So don’t make them. That’s commandment number 2 in the context of I am Yahweh your God you shall have no other gods. You can’t make images of the other gods, and you can’t make images of me. **Don’t give me a falcon head or a beetle head.**
- BRONZE SNAKE AND TABERNACLE: if you look forward though, **God does command the creation of a few images**. First, the fiery serpents that bite the people in Numbers, God instructs Moses to make a bronze serpent and put it on a pole. Which seems like it would be a violation of the second Commandment. But since God commanded it, **he must’ve made an exception here. And what do the people do with that exception?** We find out in King’s 700 years later they are burning incense to it and worshiping it and have named it **Nehushtan**. He also commands some imagery like the **cherubim in the Tabernacle**. But that’s about it, and **Nehushtan became a great example of why it was so important** that it made the Ten Commandments.
- ICONOCLASTIC: Christianity has struggled with this through its history, it’s not just Judaism. **When I look at the Catholic obsession with artifacts, it looks to me like idol worship.** The church had a few “iconoclastic” periods, meaning **“image smashing”, in the 700’s and 800’s AD, and again at the Protestant reformation**, basically all arguing the same thing: this second commandment outlawed images because we will worship them. But Christianity has a much stronger artistic history than Judaism ever has. Jews basically take it strictly: **do not make images regardless of why**, and Christianity read into it the purpose of worship: **images and art that venerate and help worship God are good**, and images and art that lead to worshipping other things than God are bad.
- OPPOSITE OF ICONOCLASTIC: modern Catholicism has an obsession with artifacts and graven images. The classic example is the **worn foot of St. Peter’s statue in St. Peter’s Basilica.** For hundreds of years, people traveled to St. Peter’s Basilica to this statue to pray to it and rub its foot, asking St. Peter to be merciful and open the gates of heaven to them. In fact, in 1857, Pope Pius IX even granted a 50-day indulgence period for people to sin with their sin pre-forgiven to anyone who kissed the statue’s foot. So many people have touched and rubbed his foot and prayed to the statue over the centuries that the toes have worn off.
  - Another recent example was at a climate-centered “save the earth from climate change” conference in 2019. At the conference, held at the Vatican, an idol of Pachamama, a “Mother Earth goddess” of fertility from the Peruvian Andes, was carried around in her boat while people bowed down to her and sang her songs, even making an appearance in St. Peter’s Basilica so the crowds could appreciate the idol while the clergy held a Catholic worship service around her.

If we look at the world around us, **what can we reasonably say about artists in general? Do we find them generally conservative evangelical Christians? Or godless woke leftists?**
  - ASK YOURSELF ABOUT YOUR TALENTS: This is a good question to ask yourself: how do people with my talents and interests usually end up? For example, at one point in my life it was becoming clear to me that I had a bent toward the intellectual. **And so I had to ask, if I become more intellectual, what effect will that likely have on me?** Will I become a better person? Are more intellectuals good? I had a sinking feeling that the answer to that question is no, and empirically so. **The dumbest, most destructive, most evil ideas of humanity almost always come from intellectuals.** So I was rightly concerned if I learn more will that make me actually dumber, more cruel, and more destructive? I set out to read about intellectuals and it turns out I was mostly right. But a key insight I found from **Thomas Sowell finally put the question to rest for me: he realized that the primary and most significant purpose of an intellectual in society is to protect the rest of society from the other intellectuals.** In other words, learning more does not necessarily turn you in to a prideful, blind moral monster. **It can, but it does not have to**. So if I have artistic talent and I follow that interest, am I likely to become antagonistic toward God too? **Correlation is not always causation: people who feel like “outsiders” to society tend to congregate in the arts** as it is historically one of the few places you could be outside the mainstream and still make a living. **And a law-based God of morality, with his 10 commandments that primarily place restrictions on behavior, is naturally going to be rejected by the person who sees themself as an outsider or a rebel**, **whose identity rests in viewing themselves as their own god, subject to no outside authority.** They may see themselves as outside authority, or they may feel like the authority pushed them outside, but either way, nonconformance to norms becomes their identity.
  - NOT ALWAYS ONE OR THE OTHER: But just because correlation is not necessarily causation doesn’t mean it can’t ever be. **If you are someone inclined to the arts, keep God square in view.**  Make God the end your art seeks to find. **And be aware that there is a pull out there in the community that will constantly attempt to pull you from Christ.** If you fail to recognize it, then it is much harder to resist. Just as if you are an intellectual, keep God square in view and understand that your community will try to pull you toward worshiping your own ego.
  - MAYBE THIS IS WHAT GOD KNEW: And perhaps God saw this and that’s a part of why this commandment is a bit ambiguous about whether it’s about visual art in general or just idols. **Because the Jews never had artistic communities, then they never had that element organized against society’s obedience to Yahweh.** And in general, the more secular a Jew is today the more they revere the arts.

That is beaten into the Jews for centuries from this second commandment. **There can be no physical representation of Yahweh. Period. Further, the Hebrew word used here as “jealous” is “qanna”, and it appears six times in the entire Bible, every single one of them only refers to God. **It is not man’s kind of jealousy.** Remember, God communicates to us using things that we can relate to, even though they are anthropomorphisms. **Exodus 20:7 > 3: name of Yahweh in vain**

- THIRD PERSON YAHWEH: this commandment uses the name Yahweh, obviously since the point is to not use the name Yahweh in vain. **But a shift happens here between the first two commandments that were spoken in the first person,** (I am commanding this, no other gods before me), and **from here on out in the Ten Commandments it is third person**: the name of Yahweh instead of “you shall not use **my** name in vain.” Instead it says “You shall not use **the name of Yahweh your Elohim** in vain”. The text doesn’t tell us why it does this, and Yahweh often switches between first and third person and the angel of Yahweh throughout the Old Testament. One explanation is obviously since this commandment is about the importance of his name, we start using his name throughout the rest of the 10 commandments.
- IN VAIN: what does it actually mean to use a name in vain? **What does vain mean?** If you call someone vain, it means they are obsessed with their own appearance. But that’s not what vain means here. **Here it means “without meaning” or “ without the appropriate meaning” or “ to no effect” or “without the appropriate effect”.** Like in Ecclesiastes, when Solomon says vanity of vanities all is vanity. It’s not a word we use often, so a lot of translations translated as “meaningless meaningless, all is meaningless”. - NO OBJECT TO THE VERB: it doesn’t say what not to steal which has posed a challenge to the rabbis and commentators over the centuries as to just what you aren’t supposed to steal. With murder, you can’t murder a person because that’s what the word means. With adultery, you can’t sleep with someone who is married, because that’s what the word means. We get a very long list of specific things we’re not supposed to covet. But with steal, it doesn’t really tell us what it’s talking about. The implication would be you cannot steal anything. Historically, it was understood primarily to mean you cannot steal a person, i.e. kidnapping. But that’s not what it says here. It says steal.
- CONTINUATION OF A THEME: do not murder means do not steal a life. Do not commit adultery means do not steal someone’s spouse. Do not bear false witness means do not steal the truth. Do not comment means do not plan to steal someone’s possessions. Then Prager had another brilliant point here that all of the ethical Commandments can be rendered in terms of do not steal. It may be the one commandment to rule them all.

Because you are taking the product of someone else’s work as your own. That’s what money is, it’s other people’s work. That’s why you want it: it’s how you get other people to do things that you do not have the time, expertise, or motivation to do. Life requires work, somebody has to do it. If you were stranded on a desert island, and you did not work, you would die. Humans are the only creatures like this: for example, God equipped cows to be able to live without work. They can just roll over and lick the grass off the ground. But for humans, our natural state is poverty. Poverty is not something that happens to us, wealth is. Poverty is what you have when you do nothing. Wealth is what you have when you store up food, build shelter, etc. We must work to survive.

Fretheim who made a great point that “theft is an attack on the dignity of human beings and their work. God dignifies human beings by giving them work to do, from which they can expect to receive some of the fruits of their labor. This is central to God’s creation intentions for humankind. Because of the Sabbath commands that we should do our work in six days, and if he does the human with dignity of purpose, when someone steals, they are stealing the holy output of honest work and desiccating it by making it an unholy output of dishonest work.
