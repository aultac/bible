# 036_Exodus12:21-14

NOPUBLISH

- THE POINT WAS PASSOVER: we learn the entire point of the plagues (and the slavery, and the sojourn to Egypt) was the eternal remembrance of passover. We’ve broken the story at the peak of the tension to cover how to remember this event.
- RECAP PASSOVER THUS FAR: take a male, unblemished lamb for 4 days, kill it at twighlight, put it’s blood on the doorpost, roast it whole, eat it all, share if needed, eat it in haste, and burn any leftovers. If you do, I will not kill the firstborn in that household. Keep a festival 7 days: clean all leaven from your house, eat unleavened bread 7 days, eat nothing leavened.

**12:21-28 > Instructions for the Big Night**

- CHILDREN ASK MEANING: in the actual Seder, they recite four questions from verses like this one. The question of the wise son, the wicked/contrary son, the simple son, and the son who does not know how to ask. **This question is the question of the wicked or contrary son, because he does not include himself in the question**: it is what do YOU mean by this service, not what do WE mean by this service. The Haggadah says he would not have been saved in the Passover because he did not include himself in the group. The other three questions are found in Deuteronomy 6:20 (wise son), Exodus 13:14 (simple son), and Exodus 13:8 (son who does not know how to ask).

- WE NOW RESUME YOUR REGULARLY SCHEDULED MASSACRE: now that the ritual is handled, the destroyer does his work. And the terrible, anguished cry happens, 50 football stadiums full of every living mom, dad, brother, sister, grandparent, and friend.

**12:29-36 > Firstborns die**

- BLESS ME ALSO: Pharaoh is finally broken. He heard the cry. He made the cry. He knows the pain the Hebrews felt when their sons were drowned. His teenage son Webensenu has died.
- DARK: No flashlights here at night. The people, all 2M+, gathered gold, packed up, and left town by firelight and moonlight. I cannot imagine organizing this. Moses had a hard job.

**12:33-42 > Plundering Egyptians and 430 years**

- BACK WAGES: the slaves end up getting paid after all. They did this BEFORE the night of the passover, probably in the preceding 14 days.
- MAP: Ramses to Succoth
- MIXED MULTITUDE: many Egyptians went with them, most likely blended families with an Egyptian dad and Hebrew mom and things like that. Those who had decided to follow Yahweh. **This causes problems later in Numbers 11:4 when the non-Hebrews stir everybody up complaining about the manna and want to go back to Egypt.** The later Rabbinic commentators also attribute the initiative for building the golden calf to these “mixed multitude”, but that is not present in the text.

**12:43-end > Passover rules**

- MIXED MULTITUDE: Given the mixed multitude with them, it is important that we establish how the rules apply to the strangers and to the Hebrews. God here will distinguish between the foreigner who does not intend to stay and the stranger who is sojourning among them staying with Hebrews and intending to stay.   **If you do not intend to stay with the Hebrews, to be a part of God‘s people, then you have not truly decided to trust Yahweh and you cannot eat the body of the lamb.**
- ONE LAW FOR STRANGERS AND HEBREWS: no one uncircumcised can eat the lamb. The one law for both strangers and Hebrews is that you must be circumcised. And what would it mean for a stranger to become circumcised? It means that they have made a commitment in their life to follow Yahweh, that they want to be a part of his covenant with the Jews, and they have demonstrated this commitment with a physical action in the physical world. **So it turns out that the chosenness of God is available to the unchosen if they want to become children of the promise.** And for the Hebrews, who refuse to keep the Passover, who choose not to eat the body of the lamb and be saved from the wrath of Yahweh, they shall be cut off from their people. **And so we learn that God‘s chosenness is not simply being a child of Abraham, but it is deciding to follow Yahweh.** And following Yahweh means accepting the gift of the lamb that saved them from the death that is God‘s just punishment for evil. There shall be one rule for the Hebrew and the stranger.
- NO BROKEN BONES AND STAYS SAME HOUSE: Why in the world would God make these rules? What does this have to do with anything?
  - PROTECTION STAYS IN THE HOUSEHOLD: the lamb died to protect the household. Its body must stay with the household that it is protecting. If it leaves the household, either it will not have the protective effect it was killed for (it would not redirect the destroyer away from a firstborn) and be effectively neutered, or it will have the protective effect but not on a household that has chosen to put their trust in Yahweh. **The body and blood of the Passover lamb will not cover anyone who has not put their trust in Yahweh, and it must be 100% effective.** This is why everything not eaten must be burned and never leave the household.
  - LAMB NEVER DECAYS: the body of the lamb must be burned if it is not consumed so that it does not ever experience decay. The leaven was cleared from the house already, and the yeast in the air would start decaying the lamb’s body if it were not burned. **The body of the sacrifice must not be allowed to decay or be re-used for any other meal or any other purpose.** The sacrificial lamb must never see the corruption of yeast which represents sin.
  - SAVED INTO A NEW LIFE OF FREEDOM: not only does the blood of the lamb save you from the consequences of sin, which is death at God‘s hand, but it saved the Hebrews into a new life of freedom from slavery with Yahweh. Everything changed for them after they put the lambs blood on their door posts.   **God was physically present with them.** And for the rest of the Bible, God primarily identifies himself as the God of freedom, the God who took them out of Egypt and slavery.
  - WHY NO BROKEN BONES? this one is less clear in context. Why leave the body whole during cooking? Why can’t you break a bone? **Is the body going to be re-used for something else after the meal?** One explanation could be that the “spotless unblemished” nature of the lamb was to carry through all the way to consumption. A lamb with a broken leg was not acceptable for sacrifice. But they had already removed the skin and blood and cooked it. So it’s blemished now. So why keep the body intact? And why make this an annual activity for every generation forever? We will see an answer to this soon….

**Modern Passover**

- DESTRUCTION OF TEMPLE: In Deuteronomy 16:5-6, God commands the Passover be kept only where he will make his name dwell, i.e. the tabernacle and then the temple. The Passover lamb was only slaughtered and roasted in the tabernacle and temple after that, and the blood was only placed on the doorposts of the tabernacle and temple. But in 70AD, the temple was completely destroyed by the Romans and has remained destroyed to this day, only to be rebuilt by the antichrist 3.5 years before the end of time. So this left the Jews in a quandary: **how do we continue our religion that is based on temple sacrifices without a temple?** So they made up a new Passover to commemorate the old one.
- JEWS HAVE COMPLETELY FORGOTTEN SIN REQUIRES BLOOD: most modern Jews do Passover and fast on Yom Kippur as repentance for sin and think they’re covered with God. Jewish evangelism often needs to start with reminding them of the Passover and the sacrificial system. God never told them blood wasn’t necessary, but after the temple destruction they just decided it must be enough to just be sorry for your sins and keep the non-sacrificial laws.
- REMOVE LEAVEN: first, they remove leaven by hiding bread or cakes in the house, kids search for it on Passover Eve (night before). Thorough house cleaning. Then ready for Passover.
- SEDER ON PASSOVER: Jews today celebrate this with a family dinner once a year. They have a ceremony that the family reads from a “Haggadah”, and everyone participates.
- HAGGADAH: from the Passover Haggadah from Rabbi Nathan Goldberg
  - SETUP:
    - Plate with Egg (represents mourning over loss of temple as the festival offering that used to be brought to the temple), bitter herbs (horseradish), shank bone of lamb, parsley, and charoset (sweet apple/nut/wine/cinnamon mixture).
    - Plate with 3 Matzah
    - 4 wine cups for each person representing 4 of the 7 promises Yahweh made to Israel
    - Filled wine cup at empty place setting for Elijah: **Read Malachi 4:5-6**
  - Seder Agenda (“Seder” means “sequence”):
    - Kadesh – Sanctification / Kiddush over the first cup of wine (your “prayer”, lean left like royalty)
    - Urchatz – Ceremonial washing of the hands (no blessing)
    - Karpas – Vegetable (usually parsley or celery) dipped in salt water (tears of Hebrews and green of spring)
    - Yachatz – Break the middle matzah; hide the larger half as the “Afikoman” meaning “after” or “desert”. They eat matzah last because the lingering flavor of the Passover should not be sweet desert or savory delicious food, but unleavened bread. So it is their desert at the end of the meal. But they hide it at this point for the kids to find later.
    - Maggid – Tell the Passover story (Four Questions, narrative, second cup of wine, **spill drop of wine for each plague** for blood of Egypt)
      - Read four questions
    - Rachtzah – Washing of the hands with a blessing before the meal
    - Motzi Matzah – Blessings over the matzah and eating it
    - Maror – Eat the bitter herbs (often with sweet charoset)
      - Read bitter herbs and every generation part
    - Korech – “Hillel sandwich” (matzah + maror, often with charoset, because of Exodus 12:8: “They shall eat the flesh that night, roasted on the fire; with unleavened bread and bitter herbs they shall eat it.” ). This was instituted in practice right before Jesus’ time, just with the lamb in the middle of the sandwich. Hillel said it was a sandwich meant to symbolize the bitterness of slavery (herbs) and freedom of redemption (matzah). Since the temple was destroyed in 70AD, they removed the middle matzah representing the lamb that they can no longer sacrifice, eating just the herbs and outer matzah in the sandwich.
    - Shulchan Orech – The meal itself
    - Tzafun – Eat the Afikoman
    - Barech – Grace after meals (Birkat Hamazon) + third cup of wine (your second “prayer”)
    - Hallel – Recitation of the Hallel (Psalms of praise 115-118, kids jump up and race to door to open and see if Elijah has come back)
    - Nirtzah – Conclusion (Songs, “Next year in Jerusalem”)

**Passover and Jesus:**

- ONLY OLD TESTAMENT USED UP TO NOW: I have not used any reference to the New Testament up to this point in the plague narrative, except for Revelation 16. Realize that everything we’ve learned has been Old Testament-only, I have not introduced any New Testament language or concepts. Now let’s consider how the Passover relates to Jesus:
  - 10th OF NISSAN: Jesus chose to enter Jerusalem on the 10th of Nissan, 4 days before Passover. He chose this, he was not forced into the date. He presented himself to the high priests at the temple just as the passover lamb was presented 4 days before Passover.
  - 14th OF NISSAN: Jesus and his people slaughter their lamb at the temple
  - 15th OF NISSAN EVENING: On the evening that started the 15th of Nissan, Jesus and his disciples were having the Last Supper, their Passover Seder. They had slaughtered their Passover lamb that afternoon and had their Seder at sundown. At that dinner, Jesus redefined the Passover forever as his body and blood as the unleavened (i.e. decay-free) bread and the wine, and he, himself, would forever be the lamb. Hence why we have no lamb meat during communion, and after 70 AD, neither did the Jews have a Passover lamb at their Seder. The Passover will continue to be practiced by both Christians and Jews every year from that time forward, to commemorate the Exodus, the killing of the Egyptian firstborn, and the death and resurrection of the final Lamb of Yahweh who takes away the sin of the world, all wrapped up into one big bucket of deep meaning.
  - 15th OF NISSAN DAY: Jesus was crucified on the day after Passover. On the day the firstborn were all found dead. He was taken by the high priests around midnight, and by the afternoon, he was dead, just as the firstborn of Egypt. On the day of the commemoration of the exact day when Yahweh took the first born sons of Egypt and saved the Hebrews, the Hebrews finished his plan of taking Yahweh‘s first born. **On the day of Passover, the first born of Yahweh had died. They took him down and placed his wrapped, whole body with a hole in the side in the tomb before the 16th of Nissan** which was the Sabbath**.**  Passover lamb was slaughtered, guts removed and cleaned through hole in the stomach, then replaced and placed on the spit to roast.
  - 16th OF NISSAN: But his body would not see corruption or decay. None of its bones were broken. None of its limbs dismembered. None of the body was taken out of the tomb, his blood was left on the wooden post of the cross, including the cross member, just as the lamb of the Passover sacrifice had its blood put on the wooden posts of the door, including the lentil above it. **Unlike the firstborn, sons of Egypt, who died and stayed dead, the first born son of Yahweh was both first born son and lamb, the body and the blood. This first born son is resurrected.**
  - NO LEAVEN: leaven is a sign of decay. It’s what happens to dead things. No leaven is allowed in the house during Passover, **because Jesus’s body never saw decay**. It’s also universally a symbol of sin in the Bible. **Jesus never sinned**, the Passover lamb is spotless and leaven is not even allowed in the house that could contaminate it.
  - NO BROKEN BONES: now that we know the Passover prefigures Jesus, it is a direct Jesus fractal, we can see the answer we couldn’t see before. Jesus never had his bones broken. His body was never dismembered and carried outside the household, outside the tomb. His body remained intact. **Why? Because he was going to be resurrected.** While God could have made him a new body, he chose instead to decree that his original body would remain unbroken and intact. They never broke his bones, even though that was what they did to the two thieves on the crosses beside him. When he was resurrected, his body still had the holes in his hands and feet and the hole in his side. If they had broken his bones or dismembered him and scattered his body parts, that could not have happened that way. **He could not have been resurrected in the same body that bore all the scars because he couldn’t walk on a broken leg and he can’t be missing an arm.** This seems to me why God made this rule for this annual, eternally-decreed ritual. This rule exists at Passover precisely because it is necessary for the resurrection of Jesus the way he wanted to do it.
  - EAT THE LAMB WITH YOUR SHOES ON YOUR FEET: just as today we are supposed to take communion with our lamps trimmed ready for Jesus at any moment.
  - ONLY THE DEATH OF THE FIRST BORN REDEEMS US: the Passover is the culmination of 10 total plagues. Nine of them did not work to redeem God‘s people from the gods of Egypt. **Only the death of the first born can do that.** And even then, they are only saved from death by eating the whole body of an unblemished young male lamb and putting his blood on their door posts. We are learning a profound truth about God in reality here. How often do you hear people say if God would just do miracles, I would believe in him? But of course, that is the kind of belief the demons have: they know he exists. God is not interested in that kind of belief. He is interested in saving faith. And **we learn here that nine of the most amazing miracles that you can imagine enacted on an entire country on an entire people were insufficient to both redeem and save.** **God is teaching us that our intuition that miracles will solve the problem is not correct.** The Egyptians knew that he was Yahweh, pharaoh knew that he was Yahweh, even Hebrews knew by this point that he was Yahweh. Miracles do that, but they don’t bring the kind of belief that gets you to stop following the regional gods or the gods of your own construction, or your god of science and nature. That takes the death of a lamb, eating its body, and washing your household in its blood.
  - SAVED FROM SLAVERY INTO FREEDOM WITH YAHWEH: just as the Passover lamb was the gateway through which the Hebrews were saved out of slavery in Egypt and into freedom with Yahweh physically living among them as the pillar of cloud and fire, so Jesus as the Lamb of Yahweh is the gateway through which we are saved out of slavery to sin and into freedom with Yahweh living directly within our bodies as the Holy Spirit.
  - PASSOVER IS REQUIRED: **Read Numbers 9:9-13.** The penalty for failing to observe passover was that you would be cut off because you would have to bear your own sin. If you reject the Passover lamb that God has provided, the path that he gave you if he saved from death, then you will bear your own sin, and you have a committed a sin just because you failed to accept the path that God gave you for salvation.
  - AFIKOMAN: remember the middle piece of bread they break and hide? Turns out, that middle piece of bread represents the lamb, that’s why they take it out of the middle for the Hillel sandwich. The Hillel sandwich takes out the middle matzah because the command in Exodus was to eat the lamb with the bitter herbs and the matzah. Since they have no lamb, they remove the middle matzah and eat just the outer two and the bitter herbs. **But they took the middle matzah, representing the body of the lamb they were supposed to be eating, and they break it and it is hidden from the Jews during the meal. The descendants of the Jews, the children, hunt and find it and bring it back to the Jews** and then they all eat it to finish the Passover meal. The body of the lamb is bread that is broken, but the Jews can’t see it until the Passover is complete. Sound familiar? The Jews have been blinded to their Messiah until the fullness of the Gentiles has come into the household of God, after which their descendants will have the veil removed and find the Afikoman again. They dutifully follow the ritual without seeing its meaning.
  - REMEMBER THE BREAK IN THE DRAMA? Now we see why. Why was the most important part of this story that the Jews remember the details of Passover forever? Why was that more important even than their freedom? It’s because this one ritual passover tells you everything you need to know about Jesus. Their job was to keep that story alive until Jesus came, so that we would know that this had been God’s plan all along.
  - IT WAS GOD’S PLAN ALL ALONG: He had a plan to bring a savior to save the entire world who had to come through somebody. So he picked a broken vessel, a feral cat, to help show the world that he loves us and wants us to be saved.
  - WHY PLAGUES ARE FIRST INTRODUCTION TO HUMANITY: for the same reason you don’t tell non-Christians that Jesus is the savior before you make them aware that they are sinners in need of a savior. The Egyptians did not think they were bad. We don’t think we’re bad. God’s first a God of punishing evil, and then a God of salvation and forgiveness. **Salvation and forgiveness are contingent on having something to be saved from and something to be forgiven for.** Plagues first, then passover.
  - COMMUNION: and to this day we Christians celebrate the Passover ourselves through communion. We eat the body of the lamb that was broken for us, and we drink the blood that was spilled for us and saved us from the destroyer who rightly punishes us for our sins, just as Jesus was doing that night of the last supper when he broke the matzah and handed it out, and he poured the third cup of wine and they drank.
