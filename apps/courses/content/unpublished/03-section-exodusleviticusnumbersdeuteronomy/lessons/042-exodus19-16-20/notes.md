**Intro to Ten Commandments**

- COUNT THE COMMANDMENTS: up to 15. WORD DOCUMENT TABLE. Jews, Protestant, and Catholics delineate them differently. Exodus 34:28 and Deuteronomy 4:13 explicitly call these the “ten words” or “ten statements”, but it never tells us where the delineations are, so each tradition decided for themselves.
- SECOND PERSON SINGULAR YOU: in English, we have the same word for “you“ when we’re speaking to one person or when we’re speaking to a group of people. Hebrew has a plural you and a singular you. Yahweh is speaking to a group of people here, but all the commands are in the singular you. In the old King James version, they use the word “thou” like thou shall not, and that gave you a much better clue as to what was going on because thou wasn’t usually something you would say to a group. The best we can do to approximate it here is saying to a plural group you would say “you all”. If I stand up here and say “you all should not murder”, do you have a completely different internal feeling and if I stare right at you individually in the eye and say “you shall not murder.”
- NEGATIVE COMMANDS: with the exception of “remember the Sabbath“ and “honor your father and mother“, the remaining eight are all negative commands. Things you are not to do. It is more important not to do bad things than it is to do good things. It’s not that you should not do good things. But if you have to choose between not murdering, and donating to charity, pick the don’t murder. Yahweh is teaching us here that but he cares about the most in the world for humanity is that we do not sin. All our good works are like filthy rags to him. Sin is the problem.
- NO ABSTRACT PRINCIPLES: the 10 Commandments is surprisingly direct. It is specific directions for behavior much more so than platitudes or principles like be a nice person, or do unto others what you want done to you, or don’t be selfish. The concrete directness leaves very little room for varying interpretations.
- SPLIT BETWEEN GOD AND MAN: the first five Commandments all bear the name of Yahweh, and his name appears nowhere in the second five. Moses had two tablets, and since Hebrew is written right to left instead of left to right like English, the “right” tablet is the first five, and the “left” tablet is the last five.
  - MAN-GOD VS MAN-MAN: The first four Commandments (right tablet) are purely obligations between man and God. The last 5 are between man and man. The remaining commandment, #5, is honor your father and mother, and it serves as a transition between the two tablets. This is because the reason you honor your father and mother is that they are in positions of God-like authority, and you honor the position. God is mentioned in #5 as well, making it the link between the two.
  - TWO-PARTY CONTRACT: For the first four, they make some sense in a two party contract. One party has obligations to the other party. But stuff thing for a second how unusual the second five Commandments are. They don’t help Scott at all. God has the second party of the contract is requiring you to treat other human beings nicely. There is no benefit to him. But he says this will be the contract. You must honor and worship me only, and what I want from you to not do these bad things to your fellow humans. This is the God we follow.
  - But now consider the first 5 as well. The other gods all required the humans to give them things in exchange for power. In this contract between men and God, does man give God anything? Does it ask for sacrifices? Does it ask for tithing? It says have no gods before me, make no grave images as gods for yourselves to worship, make sure you rest one day out of seven, and honor your parents. This is the contract that God signed with humanity. There’s nothing we can do for him that he needs. This is the God we follow.

**Exodus 20:1-3 > 1: no other gods**

- REMEMBER THE MORAL AMBIGUITY OF GENESIS? How much we were begging for God to just come tell us what was right and wrong? To just give us the law? Well here it is. The people and the earth itself tremble at the law, but it is finally here.
- START WITH WHO IS THE LAW GIVER: before giving the laws, we start with the credentials of the law giver: Yahweh who took them out of Egypt and slavery. Remember, the unique Elohim that defeats all the other Elohim outside his territory, who makes universal moral demands on all humanity, who works amazing miracles. That is who is talking. Not the creator of the universe, though. He says I am YOUR Elohim, Israel. You listen to me.
  - IS THE FIRST SENTENCE A COMMAND? Judaism has traditionally held that one of the 10 “statements” is “I am Yahweh your Elohim who took you out of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.” Is this a command? Many people think this is a command to believe in God’s existence. But as Dennis Prager points out, it cannot be that because by definition if Yahweh is saying all these words, he must exist to say them.
  - WHY NOT SAY HE IS THE CREATOR OF THE UNIVERSE? For one, that is too abstract. He is not just the creator of the universe, like Aristotle’s “unmoved mover”, he is identifying as the God who actually acts in the world because he hates evil and personally cares about his people. Remember, the Egyptians had several gods who were creators of the world. Since this is a covenant between Yahweh and his people, it does make some sense that he would identify as their tribal God, as opposed to the universal creator of everything. Because they’re only here because he said I will keep one nation as my inheritance, whereas the other nations were given as an inheritance to his other sons at Babel. But he said he would be his own son, and keep an inheritance for himself, that is this one nation in front of him.
  - WHY SAY HOUSE OF SLAVERY? Dennis Prager had an excellent set of points about why did Yahweh say “out of the house of slavery?” Why not just stop at “out of Egypt?”
    - Along the lines of this being a covenant, he opens by saying “I took you out of slavery” sort of like his part of the deal. I did this for you, now you keep my law. I am the God who took you out of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.
    - Another reason is though they don’t romanticize Egypt. Which they have already started to do. This will become the primary covenant through which they relate to God, and they cannot relate to God without remembering that Egypt was a house of slavery. It was not the place where they sat by the pot with meat and ate bread until they were full.
    - And the third reason is so that when we read this several millennia later, we also do not romanticize Egypt. If it were not for this book, we would have nothing but respect for range of Egypt. The pyramids, the Sphinx, the longevity of their civilization, their power and wealth. But because of this book, because right here in the 10 Commandments, Yahweh says I am Yahweh your Elohim, who took you out of Egypt out of the house of slavery, Egypt is actually the prototype of slavery.
    - Finally, the way identifies himself primarily as their liberator. As a God who cares about freedom. We followed God of freedom. But he identifies as the God of freedom right before the commands to take personal responsibility. He is saying I took you out of slavery and now you have to be responsible for your own actions. If you are going to be free, you must have the 10 Commandments and learn to control yourself because there is no slave master to control you now. So I am your liberator. I am a god of freedom and freedom means personal responsibility. You cannot survive in freedom if you lie and steal and murder. This is the God we follow.
- GOD SPOKE ALL THESE WORDS: the Jewish Bible scholar Benjamin Sommer points out of this phrase (God speaking) occurs around 339 times in the Bible, but there are only two places where the text does not tell us who he is speaking to. He just seems to be speaking.
  - The first is Genesis 1 in creation of these speaks the words that bring order to chaos, something from nothing.

With the 10 Commandments, we have a new creation.
- NOT GIVEN IN ISRAEL: Dennis Frager pointed out that you think that would have given the covenant with the people in the promised land when they got there why didn’t he give the 10 Commandments in Israel? Devotional understanding is that by giving them in the desert it doesn’t really belong to anyone 10 Commandments I considered universe they don’t belong to Israel. At this point is further solidified with the unique statement that God does not specify who he is talking to in this case, he must be talking to everyone. Specifically, see the universal law for his chosen people. It is their side of contract for being his chosen people. It is not just to Moses. It is not just to these people in the desert. It is to everyone who would ever be part of his chosen people. The children of the promise.

You cannot become friends outside of France. To those who would ask Yahweh to become children of the promise who would be circumcised who would follow his law. This also is the covenant for them. The idea of a covenant people defined entirely by a set of rules governing their moral order is completely unique.
- NO OTHER GODS: so what does this mean? What are other gods that would be before or instead of Yahweh? The modern view that wiped the supernatural out of the Bible assumes other gods do not exist. But this is one of the 10 Commandments, so it must be important. So they redefine other gods to mean anything in your life that is more important than Yahweh. **Exodus 20:4-6 > 2: no images or idols**

- JEWS AREN’T ARTISTS: historically, it is because of this commandment that you don’t find a lot of Jewish artists or painters anywhere. So why is one of the Ten Commandments that you can’t make images or carvings? The consensus among the commentators is because when you make images, they inevitably become worshipped. So don’t make them. That’s commandment number 2 in the context of I am Yahweh your God you shall have no other gods. You can’t make images of the other gods, and you can’t make images of me. Don’t give me a falcon head or a beetle head.
- BRONZE SNAKE AND TABERNACLE: if you look forward though, God does command the creation of a few images. First, the fiery serpents that bite the people in Numbers, God instructs Moses to make a bronze serpent and put it on a pole. Which seems like it would be a violation of the second Commandment. But since God commanded it, he must’ve made an exception here. And what do the people do with that exception? We find out in King’s 700 years later they are burning incense to it and worshiping it and have named it Nehushtan. He also commands some imagery like the cherubim in the Tabernacle. But that’s about it, and Nehushtan became a great example of why it was so important that it made the Ten Commandments.
- ICONOCLASTIC: Christianity has struggled with this through its history, it’s not just Judaism. When I look at the Catholic obsession with artifacts, it looks to me like idol worship. The church had a few “iconoclastic” periods, meaning “image smashing”, in the 700’s and 800’s AD, and again at the Protestant reformation, basically all arguing the same thing: this second commandment outlawed images because we will worship them. But Christianity has a much stronger artistic history than Judaism ever has, due to this commandment. Jews basically take it strictly: do not make images regardless of why, and Christianity read into it the purpose of worship: images and art that venerate and help worship God are good, and images and art that lead to worshipping other things than God are bad.
- THE ARTS TEND AWAY FROM CHRISTIANITY: So I think it is most likely don’t make images and sculptures to worship them, but the Jewish strict interpretation against the visual arts does have one key argument for it. **Exodus 20:7 > 3: name of Yahweh in vain**

- THIRD PERSON YAHWEH: this commandment uses the name Yahweh, obviously since the point is to not use the name Yahweh in vain. But a shift happens here between the first two commandments that were spoken in the first person, (I am commanding this, no other gods before me), and from here on out in the Ten Commandments it is third person: the name of Yahweh instead of “you shall not use my name in vain.” The text doesn’t tell us why it does this, and Yahweh often switches between first and third person and the angel of Yahweh throughout the Old Testament.
- IN VAIN: what does it actually mean to use a name in vain? What does vain mean? If you call someone vain, it means they are obsessed with their own appearance. But that’s not what vain means here. Here it means “without meaning” or “ without the appropriate meaning” or “ to no effect” or “without the appropriate effect”. Like in Ecclesiastes, when Solomon says vanity of vanities all is vanity. It’s not a word we use often, so a lot of translations translated as “meaningless meaningless, all is meaningless”. So to take the Lord’s name in vain is to use the Lord‘s name to no effect, or in a meaningless way, or too in effect that is not consistent with what his name represents.
- ULTIMATE CONSEQUENCES: he always says here that he will not forgive this sin. He will not hold him guiltless who takes his name in vain. Jesus also tells us that there is only one sin that God will not forgive: blasphemy against the Holy Spirit. So the two sins must be one and the same. Whatever it means to “take the Lord’s name in vain” must be the same thing as blasphemy against the Holy Spirit, since Jesus says every sin will be forgiven sons of man except one, and Yahweh says he will not forgive this one. So this seems like a pretty important one to figure out.
- RABBINIC UNDERSTANDING IS THE VAIN OATH: the Rabbi’s of the Talmud take this as meaning you cannot swear using God’s name falsely, and also that you cannot swear to a pointless truth using God’s name. So you cannot swear that stone is gold, but you also cannot swear that stone is stone since that is self-evident.
  - They extended this to avoid saying the name of God, Yahweh or Jehovah, at all. If you never say it, then you can’t accidentally swear by it incorrectly. Since this is the one thing God will not forgive, it’s not unreasonable to go out of your away to ensure it never happens. Not 99.9999999% sure, but 1000%.
  - They extended this to include never even writing the four letters of God’s name for fear that the Torah scroll might become worn out or accidentally thrown away which would then be throwing away the name of God. Instead they write four underscore blanks, and they use the word Adonai (Lord) when reading Torah and Hashem (“the name”) in everyday speech.
  - While I admire the care and respect they give to the name of God, Yahweh specifically told Moses that his name Yahweh was to be used to identify him forever. So in saying other things, like Hashem, or Adonai, or LORD, we are not doing what he said, which was to identify him as Yahweh.
- OMG: does this Commandment tell us that we cannot say “oh my God?” I have been on both sides of this fence in my life, so I will try to do justice to each.
- DO NOT CARRY: Dennis Prager gave the most compelling answer that I have heard on this question. The Hebrew word that we translate here “take” is “nasa”. It’s a literal meaning is “to lift up”, “to raise”, or “to carry”. It is one of the most common words in the entire Bible, and it is the word used to describe what Aaron does with his breast plate containing the 12 stones that represent Israel. It is often translated therefore as “carry”. Ancient Hebrew had plenty of words for “speak” or “tell” or “say” but it didn’t use those words. It uses raise or carry. And if you understand it as carry instead of take, it makes a lot more sense. Because in English, what does it even mean to take a name? It means to carry that name with you. So the command is, thou shall not carry the name of the Lord in vain.
  - When you are doing something and carrying the Lord’s name with you conspicuously, you have raised it up to carry it, it must not be meaningless or fail to achieve its effect. Beyond that, what have we learned about Yahweh so far in the old testament? Is the story of reality not summarized as a battle between good and evil? If you carry the name of Yahweh, you must carry it to achieve the side of the Good. If you carry it and instead achieve evil effect, you have not only carried the name of Yahweh to no effect, but you have carried it to its opposite effect. You have done evil in God’s name.
  - And nothing besmirches the name of God more than doing evil in his name. Nothing keeps non-believers from the saving truth of Christ more than when people do evil in God’s name. Just as it argues against atheism when you learn how godless Communism and Socialism committed the greatest evil mankind has ever known, it also argues against God when his followers do evil in his name. The evil of Islam in the name of Allah argues strongly that Allah is Satan and it argues strongly that he is not to be worshipped.
  - I am convinced this is the commandment and the sin that God will not forgive. When you carry his name to do evil. The blasphemy against the Holy Spirit that Jesus said was unforgivable was when those high priests who carried the authority of Yahweh in their society, saw the Holy spirit performing miracles before they’re very eyes, and they tell the people that the healing of this man’s hand came from Satan, they do evil while carrying the name of God.
- SO IS OMG OK? When I reached that conclusion, I decided that the one unforgivable sin could not possibly be accidentally saying oh my God when you were stressed or amazed. Doing evil in God‘s name, that makes sense as the one unforgivable sin. But what I didn’t fully appreciate until Ray comfort pointed it out, was just because this Commandments and the one unforgivable sin may not be saying “oh my God“, that does not mean that it is a great idea to use our English term for God so flippantly.
  - There are many wrong things that are not mentioned in the 10 Commandments. We should in fact, revere and respect God so much that we do he hesitate to use his name in meaningless context. Or worse, to use the word God to replace a curse word. Using the name of Jesus or the name of God to curse at something is as disrespectful as it gets. As Ray Comfort says, would you use your mother‘s name in place of the word for excrement, to curse? If you did, wouldn’t that be dishonoring to her? How much more so is it to the perfectly good creator of the universe?
  - And I find it fascinating that there is no other name with which we do this in modern society. Nearly All of our exclamation are either “oh my God,” or “Jesus Christ” some derivative that we use to avoid saying God or Jesus: gosh, golly, goodness, good grief, geez, gee whiz, whew lordy, lordy Moses, the list goes on….why? Why is it natural as humans in every language to involve some reference to God in our cursing and pointless nonsensical exclamations? They are actually very few words you can even use. Like “wow,” “rats,” or “yikes.” The part of us that wants to bring God into it meaninglessly is not good. That is not the purpose of God’s name. So don’t do it. But I don’t think it is the same as doing evil in God’s name.

**Exodus 20:8-11 > 4: Sabbath**

- SEVEN GROUPS CANNOT WORK: within the commandment to not work every seventh day, there are seven groups listed that cannot work. Seven is the number of completion or completeness and it also is the number of a contract or covenant, so this is sort of a covenant for all time with all the sevens.
  - FOREIGNERS CANNOT WORK: if you were a foreigner, would you consider this a blessing or a religious rule imposed on you? You should see it as a blessing. For the Israelite, God left them no loopholes. They couldn’t just force everyone else to work so they could rest on the Sabbath. The Sabbath was more than just you not working. You are not allowed to have anyone else work for you either. And between the two, you working or someone else working, it does seem worse for you to rest while you make someone else work.
  - ANIMALS CANNOT WORK: this nobody could have predicted. God specifically says animals have to rest too. Just as not abusing animals made it into the laws for humanity given to Noah, they make it into the Ten Commandments too. Of course, animals don’t work by themselves, so they would have still taken the human.
  - WHAT IF YOUR ANIMALS NEED WATER? There are exceptions to the work prohibition on the Sabbath, one being the preservation of life. If your animals need food and water, you definitely give them food and water. Cows needs to be milked. However, if you can prepare the day before to avoid having to do things on Sunday, like having a bigger water bowl, then you should do that.
- SERVANT AND SLAVE: the Hebrew word used here for male servant, and female servant is the same word for slave, “eved.” Some translations render it as slave. So why doesn’t the 10 Commandments say you shall not have slaves? Why does it say your slave must be allowed to rest once every seven days? We will find as we get into the rest of the Torah that what you think of as “slave” is indeed prohibited by the Torah. Servant or bondsman is more accurate to what the Torah is talking about. For example, the commandment not to steal is primarily a commandment not to steal a human, I.e. kidnapping. So slavery that involves kidnapping a person to force them to work for you is prohibited.
- ONLY COMMANDMENT WITH A REASON: why? Dennis Prager says it could be because it is the only commandment that is a ritual, and rituals in and of themselves do not make sense without some symbolic meaning imparted to it. So God says you rest because I rested.
  - DEUTERONOMY 5 REASON: Moses recounts the Ten Commandments in Deuteronomy 5, and they are nearly identical except for the reason given for the Sabbath. The Sabbath is still the only one given with a reason, which supports the “because it is the only ritual” view, but the reason is different. There, it is because you were slaves and now you’re free.
- SABBATH IS A MESSAGE TO THE WORLD: the most conspicuous “separateness” (holiness) of Judaism and Christianity in every society that has ever been is the Sabbath. **No other culture or religion has ever had such a thing.** It is a powerful statement to the world to voluntarily abstain from making money one day every week. The Greeks labeled the Jews as lazy even because of it.
  - SOJOURNER: imagine being a visitor to a place that really observes the sabbath? Nobody works, no fast food, no stores or gas stations. It would make an incredibly powerful impact on you.
- KEEP IT HOLY: so is working unholy and resting holy then? We said holy meant “separate in a direction”, so work is unholy? Nope. Six days you shall do all your work. Work is holy for six days, rest is holy for one.
  - SO WHAT IS “KEEPING IT HOLY” THEN? Rest is a component, but only rest is not a holy Sabbath. Studying scripture, going to church, eating with family, disconnecting (separating) from the world, these keep it holy. Keeping it holy requires work, just different work than the rest of the week. Pastor Jeremy works on the Sabbath, it’s his biggest day, but his “work” is keeping it holy and so it is what the commandment intends.
  - 1/60th OF THE WORLD TO COME: the rabbi’s in the Jewish Talmud claim that the Sabbath is just a glimpse of the world to come, specifically 1/60th. This lines up with Hebrews 4
- REMEMBER: why does it say remember? It says remember the Sabbath to keep it holy. It could be because they already knew about this from the manna earlier, so it is not a new idea. However, Dennis Prager offers another possibility that the Sabbath is already intrinsically holy, so you need to remember that it is holy, you don’t make it holy yourself. Yahweh made it holy when he stopped creation for the seventh day. Our job is to remember that holiness, we are not the source of the holiness.

**Exodus 20:12 > 5: Honor father and mother**

- SPLIT BETWEEN MAN, MAN AND MAN GOD: the first four Commandments are between men and God. The last five Commandments are between man and man. The fifth Commandment splits the two and serves as a transition. Your mother and father, our fellow human beings, but they are also elevated in a Godlike position over you. So how you choose to treat your parents is a reflection of how you choose to treat God.
  - SOBERING AS A PARENT: Dennis Prager pointed out that the parents of a young child really are in a godlike position to them. You control their food, they’re waking, they’re sleeping, their self image, their internal thought life, their interests, everything about them. Whether you like it or not. It is sobering to realize the position you are in. Do not take it lightly. Be the parent who is worthy of the honor commanded you by God.

No matter how they treat you. If you had good parents, you were among the blessed of the world. If you did not, you are far from alone. But this commandment does not have a condition. You are to honor them regardless. It is in fact, the child of the awful parent to whom this Commandment is primarily addressed. The wonderful parent is often honored without the need for a commandment.
  - HONOR GOD AS YOUR FATHER: when we apply this to God, there will definitely be times you do not understand why bad things have happened to you. God will not seem very lovable. But this commandment teaches you by practicing it in your daily life with your earthly parents that you must honor God whether you feel like he is taking care of you properly or not.
- REWARD FOR THE SAKE OF THE CHILD: the reward that God says here for following this Commandment, the only commandment with a reward, is to the child. Honoring the father and the mother is not for the sake of the father and the mother. It is for the sake of the child. In the same way, God does not need our honor, we honor God, not because he is deficient without our honor, but because it is right for us to do it. The life of the one who honors God will inevitably be longer in the land than the one who does not.
  - NO OTHER GODS IS ALSO FOR OUR SAKE: another reason this belongs on the right tablet: just as honor your parents is for the sake of the child, having no other gods, no images, not taking God’s name in vain, and remembering the Sabbath are for our sake, not God’s. He doesn’t need it, we do.

- YOUR CHILDREN SEE YOU: your children will likely remember how you treated your parents. And when they grow up, that will have a heavy influence on what it feels like to them to be normal treatment of parents. They will always tend back toward the normal. If you yell at your parents, or dismissed them, do not be surprised that is how you were adult children eventually treat you. If you want them to stay in engage with you even when they feel like you are being mean to them, then you need to stay in engage with your parents, even when you feel like they are being mean to you.
- PRACTICAL HONOR: what does it really mean in practice to honor your parents? Just sit back from a far and feel a strong sense of reverence internally for them? Does feeling honor equate to actually honoring?
  - INDIVIDUALIZED: I don’t know if there is a general rule for every person situation. Part of fulfilling this commandment is actually spending some mental effort in time figuring out a good structure for you and your parents that you are honoring them.
  - KEEP COMMUNICATION OPEN: one that I think is universal is making a point to talk to your parents frequently. Dennis Prager told the story of remembering when he was young how his father would call his mother every week and all she would do is yell at him. Because of this commandment he still called her every week. They were just some days he had to set the phone down for a minute. As a mother, don’t do this to your adult children who take the time to call you. Make it easy to keep communication open. Be aware if you are always leading with criticism.

And whenever possible, if you do disagree, be honest about just how important to the ramifications of the disagreement are and to differ to their authority when you can. Do not mock them around others, and if others mock them, stand up for them.
  - BEHAVIOR: Dennis Prager pointed out that the biggest means of honoring your parents is in your behavior when they are not around. A parent never feels more honored than when they learn their child is well behaved and kind, whether as an adult or as a kid.
  - ELIJAH AT THE SECOND COMING: do you remember how the old Testament ends? We read that a few weeks ago with the Passover because that is why the Jews leave an empty chair at the table and the kids rushed to the door to see if Elijah is there. It says that Elijah will come. It specifically says your father and your mother individually, not collectively as a sort of stand in for the word “parents”. This was not common among ancient cultures. The father was almost always honored more than the mother. But Yahweh commands both.

- HOLINESS VS ETHICS: Dennis Prager made a brilliant point about a contrast between the first tablet and the second. The first one is about holiness and the second is about ethics. The holiness tablet has a lot of explanation: I took you out of Egypt out of the house of slavery; I am a jealous god visiting iniquity on children; I will not hold guiltless he who takes my name in vain; remember Sabbath because God rested on the seventh day; honor father and mother so your days might be long in the land. But then we get to the ethics tablet and there is no explanation at all. Just “do not murder.” Period. It could have said “because all humans are made in my image” as he did in Genesis 9:6 when he first said that murderers must be put to death. But it didn’t. Why?
  - Prager’s insight was that the holiness commands require explanation but ethics do not. When you observe the Sabbath, if you do not know why you are observing the Sabbath, then it is pointless. The point of ritual is symbolism. If you don’t know the symbol, it is meaningless.
  - But ethics do not require explanation. They are not symbolic. I don’t care why you don’t murder as long as you don’t murder. You do not need to know why murder is wrong beyond “because God said so.” Practicing “do not murder” without knowing why murder is wrong beyond God’s saying so is perfectly fine. While you try to work out some explanation for why murder is wrong, don’t murder.
- ALL SECOND TABLET, COMMANDMENTS ARE NEGATIVE: Apparently, in ethics, abstaining from doing bad things is the primary concern of God and morality.
