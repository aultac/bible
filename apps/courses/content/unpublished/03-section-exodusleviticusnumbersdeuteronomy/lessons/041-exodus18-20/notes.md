**Exodus 18:1-12 > Jethro and Zipporah visit Moses**

- NOT ALL FOREIGNERS ARE EVIL: the medieval Jewish commentator Ibn Ezra makes the point that the reintroduction of Jethro, the foreigner Midianite pagan priest, right after the Amalekites attacked Israel may have been intentional as a reminder that not all foreigners are evil. In fact, Jethro is a bit of a hero in the story here.
- ZIPPORAH WAS SENT AWAY: at some point, Zipporah was sent away, because she and the boys came with Moses to Egypt on a donkey. The Torah does not tell us why she was sent away. It could be because it was too dangerous, or because Moses was too busy. Or it could be because she and Moses were not getting along. **There is some reason to believe it’s the last one, and therefore Moses did not relate sending her away in the narrative to save her dignity.** The earlier bridegroom of blood thing could be read as a negative indicator of their relationship, and Moses does later marry a Cushite woman and this makes Miriam and Aaron mad. **If I had to guess, they did not have a great marriage, and that fact is not overly clear in the text because Moses did not want to air the family’s dirty laundry for all eternity.** But it is purely a guess. But it’s not Zipporah that Moses kissed when they showed up, it’s Jethro.
- JETHRO BRINGS THEM BACK: if they had a great relationship, this was a happy reunion. If they did not, this was Jethro trying to get them out of his house. He is successful if Moses’ excuse was he’s just too busy, and Jethro is about to solve that for him.

**Exodus 18:13-end > Hierarchical judiciary**

- BASIS OF JEWISH LAW AND WESTERN JUDICIARY: an ancient pagan Midianite, priest. Yet more proof this story was not made up. If the Jews or Moses had made it up, it would’ve been Moses‘s genius idea.
- HOW TO TAKE CRITICISM: Moses does stand out here though, and how he takes Jethro’s criticism. How many of us, when honestly criticized with a good idea can just say “**you know what that’s a good idea, you are right.**”
  - MOSES CHOSE TO INCLUDE THIS EMBARRASSING STORY: to Moses’ and the Jews’ credit, they chose to relate this story. It’s not actually necessary: **there was no point before this where we were noticing Moses couldn’t keep up with governing.** It makes Moses look bad, and yet it’s still here because it was the right thing to do to attribute a good solution to a tough problem to Jethro. Proof nobody made this story up.
- JUSTICE VERSUS COMPASSION: properties of a good judge: able, fears God, trustworthy, and no bribes. Dennis Prager points out here that one glaring omission in the properties of a good judge here is compassion. In our day, our justice system has been completely the neutered because we have forgotten that most often, justice and compassion are opposites. **Justice is inclusive: when justice is done, both parties receive justice.** The perpetrator is punished and he receives justice, and the victim is acknowledged and also receives justice. But compassion is not inclusive, it is exclusive.**You cannot be compassionate to both sides in a dispute.** Compassion to one is cruelty or injustice to the other. So Moses and Jethro were far more morally intelligent than the judges of our day, know that rendering justice does not necessarily include men who are compassionate. There is a place for compassion, but it is not the courtroom.
  - YAHWEH SHOWS US COMPASSION AND JUSTICE: Yahweh show us how the two can live together: he is the judge, sitting at the bench, rendering a just verdict of death for sin. **But Yahweh then takes off his judge robes, comes down from the bench to give us a hug, and then serves the sentence he has just pronounced if we are willing to repent and accept his gift.** Justice is served, but he has compassion on who he wills. Yahweh has solved a seemingly unsolvable problem of how God can be both forgiving and just.

**Exodus 19:1-6 > Yahweh’s conditional promise to make Israel priests**

- FULFILLMENT OF THE SIGN: **Read Exodus 3:10-12.** They are finally here, at Horeb (Sinai). God fulfilled his sign he originally gave to Moses. **When you worship me on this mountain, then you will know**.
- EAGLE’S WINGS: proof that not everything God says is literal, sometimes it’s poetic. He did not fly them out of Egypt on an eagle. The ancient Jewish Rabbi Rashi from around 1100 AD wrote that this phrase comes from the fact that an eagle carries its young on its wings instead of in its talons because it knows it is king of the birds and nothing can fly above it, so it protects the young that way. Turns out, this doesn’t seem to be true. **Eagles don’t carry their young on their wings, Rashi was wrong**. God is just saying “I flew you out on a big bird’s wings”, which has a better poetic picture than “I dragged you out with a big bird’s talons”.
- REMINDER OF CARE BEFORE THE LAW: God is about to give them the 10 commandments and the law. But before he does that, he reminds them of how he took care of them.
- CONDITIONAL COVENANT: this covenant is preceded with a condition: “If you will indeed obey my voice and keep my covenant….” So if they do not, they have broken the covenant and Yahweh can void it.
  - UNCONDITIONAL COVENANT: Commentators say this is the first “conditional” covenant, whereas the Abrahamic covenant was “unconditional”: God said he would give Abraham many descendants, they would inherit the promised land, they would be sent to Egypt as slaves but come back to the land. **Back in Genesis 15, God passed through the halves of the animals in that weird ceremony and signed his name to the contract, and it was re-iterated in Genesis 17** (inclusio). Then, after Abraham was willing to sacrifice Isaac in Genesis 22, the angel of Yahweh confirms that “because he obeyed my voice”, he will surely do what he said and added that the descendants will be a blessing to the nations of the world.
  - WAS THAT REALLY UNCONDITIONAL? The angel of Yahweh doesn’t tell us what would have happened if he withheld Isaac, but he does seem to indicate it was “cemented” when he listened to the voice of Yahweh.
  - CIRCUMCISION: But most importantly, in Genesis 17, God explicitly puts a condition on the promise: **circumcision. Critically, this was a ritual condition, not a moral one.** **If you don’t cut off, then you have chosen to be cut off.**
  - CUT OFF: Esau was not child of the promise, and 19 things later can get you “cut off”
  - UNCONDITIONAL IN GENERAL, CONDITIONAL IN SPECIFIC PEOPLE: the promise is unconditional in general: it indeed happened to “Israel.” But a particular descendant had conditions always. They could be a child of the promise, or they could reject Yahweh and be cut off from their people, no longer a child of the promise. Regardless of their choice, God’s promises for “Israel” would happen.
  - SEVEN PROMISES IN EXODUS: these promises were not made with explicit conditions: he said he would redeem them take them out of Egypt and give them the promised land. But again there’s a difference between the nation and the specific people. For the nation of Israel, this happens even though they do not hold up their end of the bargain. But the specific people who escaped Egypt do not make it to the promised land except for Caleb and Joshua. **So the unconditional part of the promise is in general, the circumcision, obedience, and primarily trust in Yahweh are required to keep from being cut off from that promise.**
- CHOSEN FOR WHAT? Yahweh tells them he chose them to be his treasure and to be priests to the nations. He does not tell them that he chose them because they were amazing, he tells them he chose them to do something for him. He rules the whole world, and their job is to bring the knowledge of him to the rest of the world. **They are to create the conduit by which the world learns about and interact with Yahweh.** That is what a priest is supposed to d: they bring the request of the people to God. The prophet goes the other direction: they bring the messages of God to the people.
- TENSION WITH HOLY PRIESTS: Dennis Prager points out that there is an implicit tension in what God tells Israel he has chosen them for. They are supposed to be a holy people, which means separate from the pagan nations around them, but they are also supposed to be priests to those nations. **So there will forever be in the rest of Jewish history, constant shifting forward and backward between separateness and involvement with the nations to which they are supposed to be priests.** The more they close themselves off to stay holy and perform God‘s rituals, the more the people around them distrust and hate them, and do not allow them to be their priests. But the more they engage with the communities, they are supposed to serve, the less separate and holy, they become, with their children getting absorbed into the culture around them.
- NEXT STEP IN GOD’S PLAN TO DEAL WITH SIN: this creation of a nation of priests is the next major phase in God’s plan to deal with the sin that Adam and Eve brought into the world. Remember how we got here: first, God made it easy not to sin. **He only made one rule.** We broke it, and in breaking it, we got the next attempt to deal with sin: **the conscience.** Now we have an internal feeling or a little voice in our heads to make sure we know right from wrong. But we can overrule it, we can mute it, we can rationalize it away. With the conscience, the second brother kills the first brother. Eventually, the whole world is so bad all their thoughts are evil all day long and God wiped them all out with the flood, except for the best man in his generation, Noah and his family. **Now phase 3, is that all people will be descended from the genes of a good man.** He ends up passed out drunk and naked not long after the flood, and curses his grandchild Canaan forever. Good genetic stock doesn’t seem to work. Now we receive the culmination of phase 4: **one nation will act as priests the other nations**, to give everyone on earth a conduit to God and carry his Torah to them.
- REVOLUTIONARY DEMOCRATIZED ACCESS TO THE DIVINE: this was completely new in human history. Imagine being an Israelite. The only religion you know is Egypt. In Egypt, the priestly class and economic classes are hereditary. Born a slave, die a slave. The priestly class was not available to anyone not born in the right position. And now, this God is telling you that you are all priests to the nations, and sojourners can choose to get circumcised and join you so it’s not just hereditary, and your job is to democratize access to the divine. God is not just for the upper class.   **You who were a slave are to make Yahweh available to all the nations of the world.** Later Levites within Israel are hereditary, but all Israel is priests to the nations.

**Exodus 19:7-9 > People promise to do what Yahweh commands**

- FIRST GOOD THING MOSES CAN SAY TO GOD: finally, he has something good to report. The people said they would do what God wants.
- SOMETHING IS MISSING HERE: at this point, **the people have signed a contract without reading it.** God has not yet told him what the commands are and they have already said “oh yes, we will do it.” Whatever “it” is. God says, “I took care of you before because I chose you, now you need to follow my law and fulfill the purpose I have for you to continue to get the care,” and the people said “yes”. In a sense, they didn’t have a lot of choice. Before, Yahweh was giving them grace. **But now he is withdrawing his freely-given grace and the people are agreeing to the law instead.** The Abrahamic covenant was pure grace, God chose and gives freely, but the Mosaic covenant is law. The New Covenant in Christ returns us to the Abrahamic covenant of grace.
- LOVE FIRST, THEN FEAR: at this point, the Israelites have accepted the covenant with God after he told them he took care of them in love by carrying them out of Egypt on eagle’s wings. They signed the contract before the fear ever shows up at Sinai in the cloud of thunder.
- THICK CLOUD: I found this verse helpful in explaining what’s about to come. The purpose of the thick cloud and the coming appearance of God is **so that the people understand God speaks through Moses and Moses alone.**  Remember that as we get into the preparation for God’s coming.

**Exodus 19:10-15 > Prepare for the mountain, take a bath**

- HOLY ALSO MEANS CLEAN: “Holy” means “separate”, but it doesn’t only mean “separate”. A clean person is separate from a person covered in mud, but we would only apply “holy” to the clean person even though the muddy person is also separate. **Holiness is separateness in a particular direction.**
- FIRST COMMAND AFTER JEWS PROMISE TO FOLLOW HIM: **is to wash your clothes.** You can imagine if you were in God’s position looking down over these 2 million slaves who were chased out of Egypt and wandered through the desert for 3 months with sometimes limited water, just how filthy the entire group would look to you. They left the washing machines back in Egypt. **It makes some sense from that picture that God would say “The first time I appear to a large group of humans, you are going to look presentable.** You will be “holy” from the world around you, cleansed of the dirt and road grime.”
  - DON’T JUST WEAR ANYTHING YOU WANT TO CHURCH: and this verse is why I try to dress differently on Sunday. At church, we come to Sinai to meet with God. And the first thing he wants from us is to look presentable. And you can see how this holiness works today. Imagine walking into a restaurant after church and seeing two families eating: one in their regular clothes, largely unkempt, and one family dressed up, showered, hair combed. **You immediately assume the dressed up family came from church, and the other didn’t.** This is how holiness works. It is a clear signal to others around you that you are a Christian and you take meeting with God seriously.   **You have to be careful not to be self-righteous in your cleanliness, as if you are somehow not just as much a sinner as anyone else, you are after all the Israelite slave who only cleaned up because God told you to, but you should also recognize that it is good to do what God wants of you.**  It is a blessing in your life to gain the dignity of cleanliness and wearing your “Sunday best.”
  - IF YOU HAVE TO CHOOSE: it is better to come to church grungy than not at all. But rarely does this condition persist, where you really have no choice but to come without showering or in your best clothes.
- BAPTISM, DESERT, SANCTIFICATION: Paul told us that the Israelites were baptized into Moses in first Corinthians 10, and I made the point that after the baptism comes the desert, where God tests you. **Part of that testing is sanctification**. Cleaning you up to get ready to meet God.
  - DO NOT GO NEAR A WOMAN: Leviticus 15:19 later tells us that anyone who has sex must bathe and is unclean until evening. This is in contrast to many pagan gods that were favorites of the people in which sex was part of worship worshiping them. **Yahweh says sex is not going to be a part of worshiping him.** No sex while you prepare to meet with Yahweh.
  - THREE THINGS COMMANDED TO GET READY: There are only actually three things commanded here: **wash your clothes, set limits around the mountain so you don’t touch it, and don’t have sex**. I’m not sure if anybody could have guessed this in advance. It does seem somewhat arbitrary of all the things God could have commanded. He could have said “everyone hug their neighbor, give your children some gifts so we’re all in a good mood, sing the song of Moses, then we’ll get started.” Remember, we are reading this to learn about Yahweh because we don’t know things about him. **If something seems unexpected, that is a thing that we did not know about him. Cleanliness matters, and sex makes us unclean.** Probably not what most of us would like, but that’s what’s here.
  - UNCLEAN VS SINFUL: when we get to Leviticus, we will learn extremely well what exactly it means to be clean and unclean. You just have to take my word for it now that the primary factor of clean and unclean is symbolism of life and death, Yahweh vs. Egypt. **Things that represent life are clean, things that represent death are unclean.** The primary thing that Christianity has failed to teach us because it’s so often skips the Old Testament, is **we think unclean means sinful.** **It is a fundamental misunderstanding.** Sex within marriage is not sinful, but there are things that make you unfit to stand before God for a short period of time. They are sinful in the context of doing them shortly before entering the temple or meeting with God. **Like going to the bathroom is not sinful in the bathroom, but if you did it in the middle of the sanctuary, that would be sin.** So don’t take away from this that God doesn’t want husbands and wives to have sex, but I understand that if you’re in church and that’s all you’re thinking about, you need to focus better. As Dennis Prager said, baseball managers often ask this of their team: don’t have sex the night before the big game so that you are focused with all of your energy on the game. If it’s good enough for baseball, it’s good enough for God to ask before the most momentous event in history since the flood.
- THREE DAYS UNTIL YAHWEH COMES: the last time Yahweh came, an entire nation’s first born died. Three days of anticipation and preparation would build the tension to a climax. **He clearly intends to scare them, and that is exactly what happens.**
- SHOT OR STONED: they did not have guns, this “shot” is shot with an arrow. Why? **Because you can’t run up on the mountain to get him**, or you’re touching the mountain now too. So you shoot him from a distance. Man or beast.
- WHY NOT TOUCH THE MOUNTAIN? God is pretty clear, nobody can touch the mountain. Nobody can try to go up. One reason is that anyone who sees God will die, Moses included. **But Moses is going up and he survives. So that must not be it.**
  - CAPITAL PUNISHMENT: So why capital punishment for this offense? The degree of the punishment is usually an indicator of the degree of the offense. This must be pretty serious.
  - BLIND OBEDIENCE: One aspect is just that God said don’t do it, so **if you do it in this context, it’s not on accident.** You are deliberately disobeying a direct order from God. So that could be part of it.
  - SURELY THERE WAS A REASON: HOLY GROUND? But why would God make this direct order? Why not allow other people to come up the mountain? One answer could be the people are not holy. If God is on the mountain, it is holy ground. To bring sin onto holy ground is an offense, because the only thing that pays for sin is blood. **But Moses goes up on the mountain, and he’s had them sanctifying themselves for 3 days.**  So the sanctification is enough for them to hear from God, but not enough to go up the mountain. So we still don’t know why they have to clean themselves but they can’t go up.
  - THE TEXT ACTUALLY GIVES US A REASON: But it struck me this week: there is another more practical reason for this that is right there in the text. Why did God say he was coming in the thick cloud? It was precisely so all the people could see that he spoke through Moses and Moses alone and they should believe Moses. **So anybody who tries to go up the mountain is challenging Moses’ authority.** They are trying to say “well maybe God can talk through me too, and I can be in charge like Moses.” In fact, this happens later with his sister, Miriam and Aaron and God has to punish them. Miriam’s skin turns leprous and she has to beg Moses to pray to God for her after complaining that she and Aaron should be able to lead to the people too just like Moses.
  - WHY IS SINGLE MEDIATOR IMPORTANT? So why would God find it so important to make sure that there is not an oligarchy talking to him, but rather a single mediator? Because Moses points forward to Jesus. **After Jesus comes, he is the only way to God. There is one mediator between God and man.** You can’t elect yourself mediator. Anyone else who tries to touch the mountain will die. The elders and Aaron can come up later. But first, we have to know Yahweh speaks through his one chosen mediator and that is how he intends for reality to operate once Jesus, the “prophet like Moses” comes.
  - BEG JESUS TO GO UP THE MOUNTAIN FOR US: Just as God intended to scare the Israelites with his awesome presence so they would beg Moses to be their mediator, so **we should fear Yahweh enough that we beg for Jesus to stand before the great and awesome Yahweh so that we do not have to.** It would be terrifying to have no mediator. And that is exactly the fate of those who rejected Jesus as their mediator. **They think they can touch the mountain themselves and survive.**

**Exodus 20:1-3 > 1: no other gods**

- REMEMBER THE MORAL AMBIGUITY OF GENESIS? How much we were begging for God to just come tell us what was right and wrong? To just give us the law? Well here it is. The people and the earth itself tremble at the law, but it is finally here.
- START WITH WHO IS THE LAW GIVER: before giving the laws, we start with the credentials of the law giver: Yahweh who took them out of Egypt and slavery. Remember, the unique Elohim that defeats all the other Elohim outside his territory, who makes universal moral demands on all humanity, who works amazing miracles. That is who is talking. Not the creator of the universe, though. He says I am YOUR Elohim, Israel. You listen to me.
  - IS THE FIRST SENTENCE A COMMAND? Judaism has traditionally held that one of the 10 “statements” is “I am Yahweh your Elohim who took you out of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.” Is this a command? Many people think this is a command to believe in God’s existence. But as Dennis Prager points out, it cannot be that because by definition if Yahweh is saying all these words, he must exist to say them.
  - WHY NOT SAY HE IS THE CREATOR OF THE UNIVERSE? For one, that is too abstract. Remember, the Egyptians had several gods who were creators of the world. Since this is a covenant between Yahweh and his people, it does make some sense that he would identify as their tribal God, as opposed to the universal creator of everything. Because they’re only here because he said I will keep one nation as my inheritance, whereas the other nations were given as an inheritance to his other sons at Babel. But he said he would be his own son, and keep an inheritance for himself, that is this one nation in front of him.
  - WHY SAY HOUSE OF SLAVERY? Dennis Prager had an excellent set of points about why did Yahweh say “out of the house of slavery?” Why not just stop at “out of Egypt?”

But because of this book, because right here in the 10 Commandments, Yahweh says I am Yahweh your Elohim, who took you out of Egypt out of the house of slavery, Egypt is actually the prototype of slavery.
    - Finally, the way identifies himself primarily as their liberator. As a God who cares about freedom. We followed God of freedom. If you are going to be free, you must have the 10 Commandments and learn to control yourself because there is no slave master to control you now. So I am your liberator. I am a god of freedom and freedom means personal responsibility. You cannot survive in freedom if you lie and steal and murder. This is the God we follow.

**Exodus 20:7 > 3: name of Yahweh in vain**

So this seems like a pretty important one to figure out.
- IN VAIN: what does it actually mean to use a name in vain? What does vain mean? If you call someone vain, it means they are obsessed with their own appearance. But that’s not what vain means here. Here it means “without meaning” or “ without the appropriate meaning” or “ to no effect” or “without the appropriate effect”. Like in Ecclesiastes, when Solomon says vanity of vanities all is vanity. It’s not a word we use often, so a lot of translations translated as “meaningless meaningless, all is meaningless”. So to take the Lord’s name in vain is to use the Lord‘s name to no effect, or in a meaningless way, or too in effect that is not consistent with what his name represents.
- DO NOT CARRY: Dennis Prager gave the most compelling answer that I have heard on this question. The Hebrew word that we translate here “take” is “nasa”. It’s a literal meaning is “to lift up”. It is one of the most common words in the entire Bible, and it is the word used to describe what Aaron does with his breast plate containing the 12 stones that represent Israel. It is often translated therefore as “carry”. And if you understand it as Carey instead of take, it makes a lot more sense. Because in English, what does it even mean to take a name? It means to carry that name with you. So the command is, thou shall not carry the name of the Lord in vain. When you are doing something and carrying the Lord’s name with you, it must not be meaningless or fail to achieve its effect. Beyond that, what have we learned about Yahweh so far in the old testament? Is the story of reality not summarized as a battle between good and evil? If you carry the name of Yahweh, you must carry it to achieve the side of the Good. If you carry it and instead achieve evil effect, you have not only carried the name of Yahweh to no effect, but you have carried it to its opposite fact. You have done evil in God’s name. And nothing these merch is the name of God more than doing evil in his name. Nothing keeps non-believers from the saving truth of Christ more than when people do evil in God’s name. I am convinced this is the commandment and the sin that God will not forgive. When you carry his name to do evil. The blasphemy against the Holy Spirit that Jesus said was unforgivable was when those high priests who carried the authority of Yahweh in their society, saw the Holy spirit performing miracles before they’re very eyes, and they tell the people that the healing of this man’s hand came from Satan, they do evil while carrying the name of God.
