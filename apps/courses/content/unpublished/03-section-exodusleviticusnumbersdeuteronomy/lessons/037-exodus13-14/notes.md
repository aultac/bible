**Exodus 13:11-16 > You owe your firstborn to Yahweh**

- CONSECRATE AND REDEEM DEFINITION: “redeem” comes from Latin “redimure”, “re” meaning “again” or “back” and “dimure” meaning purchase. It means to buy back. So a first purchase is not a redeem. Redeem is to buy something back that you lost or already had. Consecrate is “con” (meaning with) “secrate” is from “secrare" meaning “sacred” or “holy”. So it means “with sacredness”. It is a dedication of something to be used for God.
- CONSECRATE FIRST BORN: you don’t get to fully keep your first born. They belong to God. The first born were to perform the religious duties for God, but God changes this requirement in Numbers 3:11-13 to just be Levites, and fixes the firstborn redemption price at 5 shekels in Numbers 18.
  - WHY DOES ISRAEL OWE HIM THEIR FIRSTBORN? As Mike Winger put it, God is saying “Israel, you owe me a firstborn.” Why do they owe their firstborn forever? God’s justice demands it for sin because he punished some sinners (Egypt) and not others (Israel). **The firstborn of Egypt had to die for their freedom.** So Israel forever owes Yahweh their firstborn.
  - REDEEM OPTION: But God does not want humans to kill all their firstborn even though justice demands it. So he offers them an out: someone can redeem you. **Someone else can buy the firstborn back from God in lieu of him or her actually having to die or spend their life serving God in religious duties overseeing the tabernacle or temple.** The firstborn animals were sacrificed, and a lamb can take the place in death as a sacrifice for an unclean animal like a donkey.
  - GOD IS NOT EXEMPT FROM HIS OWN LAW: he also gives his firstborn son to Yahweh, just as all the Israelites have done since the Exodus. But we didn’t give him the option to buy back his son for 5 shekels. We didn’t say “ok, we’ll stop sinning, he doesn’t need to die. Just give us 5 shekels and we’ll stop sinning.” No, he had to go through with it all the way to death. But now God’s household, of which we became a part when we repented and trusted in Jesus, has its firstborn already consecrated to Yahweh. Because of Jesus, we no longer owe firstborns to God. Instead, we just owe him everything.

**Exodus 14:15**

- WHY DO YOU CRY TO ME: this is confusing. Moses hasn’t cried out in the narrative. The people have been crying, but not to Yahweh. Moses has been telling them to trust Yahweh. Moses has already been told that they would win, he just hasn’t been told how. So there is not clear explanation for this verse. Is he talking to Moses as if he is representative of the crying people or is he talking to Moses as an individual? Has Moses cried out to him already and it’s just not recorded, or is he about to cry out because the people asked him to and God is preemptively stopping him? Is it a rhetorical question that basically means “don’t cry out to me, get moving instead?” Some say this means there is a time for prayer and a time for action, it is instructive and not condemnatory. But it can also mean God already told Moses what to do and he is just delaying, and God is correcting him with a question. In either case we have to imagine things that are not explicitly in the text. It’s just not clear to me from the text. Regardless of sentiment, Yahweh is telling Moses move forward and part the sea.

They didn’t have to see the Red Sea parting to know he existed. No, this kind of belief is that now they start to trust Yahweh. He said he would take them out of Egypt, and they didn’t believe (trust) him. Now they do.

If it’s just wind, then maybe there’s a chance they get through. He continues his pattern of performing miracles with nature as his tools.
