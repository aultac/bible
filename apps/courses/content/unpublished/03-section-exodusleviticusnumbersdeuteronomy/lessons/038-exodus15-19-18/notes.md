NOPUBLISH

- Baptism symbolism coming through the Red Sea. Chased by the army of sin, washed away by water. Fractal. Verse where it says they were baptized into Moses. Baptism into Jesus walks through the water and comes out the other side. We should feel like singing the song of Moses when we’ve been saved. Reality is after baptism comes the desert, which is full of Marah’s and Elim’s.

- YAHWEH GETS GLORY WHEN EVIL PEOPLE DIE: Dennis Prager made this point. Yahweh says he will get glory over Pharaoh and his host. We are to glorify him when justice is done and evil people die. Do we? **Do we glorify him when evil dies?**
  - DISBELIEF IN GOD BECAUSE OF EVIL: many unbelievers point to evil in the world as a reason to not believe in the existence of Yahweh. Implicit in their claim is that Yahweh should be either not allowing evil at all, or at least punishing it in this world directly. So they see evil as a reason not to give glory to Yahweh. And yet these same people claim the Yahweh of the Old Testament is a judgmental moral monster precisely when he does act to punish evil. So they also disbelieve in Yahweh when he does punish evil, like killing Pharaoh.
  - THOU SHALT NOT JUDGE: and why are they angry at Yahweh when he punishes evil? Because they believe it is morally wrong to judge someone. They think they got that idea from Jesus, but what Jesus said was it is morally wrong to judge someone HYPOCRITICALLY. First stop the behavior yourself, then you darn well better judge the other person who is doing the evil. Ironically, these people claiming to be following Jesus are doing precisely what Jesus commanded not be done: they are judging someone else for judging, which by definition is judging hypocritically.
  - BUT ISN’T PHARAOH A SINNER LIKE US? But isn’t the reason we shouldn’t judge because we are sinners too? And we don’t want Yahweh to judge sinners because then he’d judge us too? Doesn’t the Lord’s prayer say “and please forgive us our sins, as we forgive those who sin against us”? Isn’t that what I mean when I say to read the Bible as the perpetrator, to read it as Cain not as Abel? Not quite. Yes we are all sinners. Yes we all should want salvation. But not by discounting sin in order to avoid judging. We don’t say well I’m bad and Cain was bad too, and I want to avoid punishment so we should let both me and Cain off the hook. Discounting sin to be less than it is discounting what Jesus did for us. No, we have to stare it straight in the face and recognize that the only thing that pays for sin is blood. We should look at our fellow sinners and wish for their salvation through repentance, through the end of our sinning, not through avoiding judging sin for what it is. It is in fact evil to avoid calling evil what it is.
  - SHOULD WE PITY PHARAOH? Maybe. We should wish that he had not done the evil in the first place. But once he has done the evil, we should glorify Yahweh when Pharaoh is defeated. We must remember the pain of Pharaoh’s victims when Yahweh punishes his evil. Too often people cry foul when murders are put to death or rapists go to long prison sentences, feeling pity for the perpetrator instead of their victims. They do not remember the pain of the victims. When Yahweh washes the sea over Pharaoh’s army, the only thing you should be thinking about is all the evil Pharaoh and Egypt did to the Hebrews. Tearing baby boys from their mother to throw them in the river. Beating them, enslaving them, killing them as the slave master was doing to the Hebrew, breaking their spirit.

- 500,000-1M GALLONS: about 10-20 semi tankers full of water for only 1-2 quarts per person per day.
- THE TRUST LASTED A WHOPPING 3 DAYS: We are Israel in this story. You know if you were there, you would have been complaining just like them. I thought Yahweh was going to take care of us? My whole family is really thirsty, why did he lead us here? Obviously if he really cared for us, he would prevent us from feeling any pain or discomfort, my children and I should not feel this thirst. But he lead us here where the thirst is unbearable and the water is undrinkable. He is not doing his job!
- EMOTIONAL ROLLER COASTER: the roller coaster ride is not done yet. We’re singing to the Lord, three days later we’ve had no water and we’re in the desert, then we finally find water, “ we are saved, oh thank you, Yahweh.“. “Kids, it’s OK, they found water. We can see it in the distance.” We taste it, and it is undrinkable. Now what?
- WHY DID GOD LEAD THEM HERE? Clearly as a test of their faith. On purpose. Clearly they failed. Would they trust him if the going got rough? But he proves himself faithful yet again anyway.
- NONE OF THE EGYPTIAN DISEASES: wait, so you were going to put the Egyptian diseases on us? So if we don’t stay faithful to you and do what you say, we are the same as the Egypt Egyptians?
  - WHO DID HE SAY IT TO? Hebrews? Nope: the mixed multitude. So first, it only appplied to people who followed Yahweh out to the desert
  - MISUNDERSTANDING: the medieval commentator Rashi claims that this means studying and following the Torah is protection against illness. There are plenty of prosperity gospel preachers who points to this verse and say if you just have enough faith you or your family will be healed. But that is not what it says. It specifically says the disease is God brought on Egypt, it is specifically talking to the Hebrews who left Egypt to whom he is going to give his law, and it specifically says you have to follow my law. We know that every human is a sinner, so none of the Hebrews followed his laws. It says nothing about having faith in him, it says you must diligently listen and keep his laws. By faith, we are saved from death into eternal life, but we are obviously not granted immunity from disease by faith. We don’t earn it as if faith is an accomplishment we can boast about. When your child gets sick and dies, it is not because you lacked faith in God. If that were true, then there have been no true Christians since Jesus came because everyone got sick at some point. This does not mean have enough faith in God and you won’t get sick. What it means instead is the Hebrews are destined to get sick. As are we.
  - I AM YOUR HEALER: strangely, God ends this sentence that he is their healer (or physician), as if he can fix the disease after they get it, not how he started this sentence where he wouldn’t give them the disease in the first place. Because he knows they’re going to sin, so the prevention of disease is not available to them. What they need is a healer, someone who can fix them despite their sin.
  - THE BEST DOCTORS GO TO HELL: Dennis Prager pointed out a really interesting Talmudic statement that’s the best doctors go to hell. The explanation for it is this verse right here. Who were the people doctors before this statement? It was the Egyptian priests. Now God is showing them in no uncertain terms they do not need the Egyptian priests for their health. The best doctors go to hell because that profession is entirely too easy to see yourself as a God. To see yourself as the one who heals your patients. Just ask the ex-wives of the best doctors, they will tell you.. it might be a very useful thing for a doctor to put this verse up in their waiting room quote “ for I am Yahweh, your healer“
- WHY A LOG? Umberto Cassuto, an Italian commentator, makes the point that God could be showing the Hebrews here specifically they do not know how to live without his instruction. Nobody would’ve guessed throw the log in. He is preparing them to receive his rules and rituals and the Torah. You don’t know how to live unless you receive instruction from me.
  - A DIFFERENT LOG APPEARS LATER: another piece of wood appears later in our story, in about 1400 years. Like this log, due to a miracle that God works, it is able to make bitter things sweet. The bitterness of crucifixion and the sweetness of the resurrection of Jesus. Apply the cross to bitterness and it becomes sweet living water. This may be why something so seemingly random (“throw a stick in the water”) was done: it pointed forward to the cross.

  - CONTROLLED REBELS: The child rebelling and doing everything their parents don’t want them to do are being just as controlled by their parents as the child doing everything their parent wants.   - LIFE ON/OFF LEASH: Just as David Wood said in his conversion story, that as a true psychopath he was intentionally doing things that society disapproved of, including attempted murder, but he didn’t want to think about the fact that doing the opposite of what society wanted was just as meaningless as doing what society wanted. Don’t approve of their actions, don’t let them affect your love for them, but don’t let them control the situation by manipulating your counterwill.
  - COUNTERWILL IS NATURAL: understand that it is your nature to react to hurt with anger, to poverty with despair, to mockery with resentment. It is natural to be a slave to your circumstances. But the God of the 10 Plagues and the Red Sea crossing showed us there is something separate from nature. You don’t have to be a slave to your nature.

**Exodus 16:4-8 > Yahweh says Meat and Manna will test the people**

- WHY NOT TELL THE PEOPLE IT IS A TEST? Yahweh tells Moses, but he doesn’t tell the people being tested. Turns out, if you tell a test subject they are being tested, it can change the outcome, it’s called the “Hawthorne Effect.” If I tell you were are going to study whether blue or green colored soap in the bathrooms causes more handwashing, and we put the regular soap in one bathroom as a control, blue in another, and green in another, we’ll most likely find all soap use and hand washing increased, especially if there were a camera at the sink or a human observer taking notes. Telling people about a test in advance can affect the test (but not always predictably). Just as Pharaoh had to have his heart hardened so he could exercise his free will in the face of plagues, God doesn’t want to influence their free will by telling them they are being tested, so he just tells Moses.
- TESTS ARE ALSO TEACHERS: Tests also can teach us things. In college, there were many times that the intense focus of the exam led me to a much deeper understanding of the topic while I was actually taking the test. You see what’s really important to the teacher at that time by what they choose to test you on, and the teacher usually wants to see if you can figure out how to apply the general principle to other problems. So tests are not only evaluative, they are instructive.
  - As one professor related to our class, he once got a note from a student after the class was over thanking him for giving the class. The note included this cleverly veiled complaint: “In this class, I saw a lot of things I had never seen before. Many of them on the final exam.”
  - PROSPERITY, LIKE MIRACLES, DOESN’T WORK: As we will see soon when the Israelites go from complaining about not having food and water to complaining about the food Yahweh gives them, Yahweh’s test is teaching us that even if he does provide for our every need, it we still won’t trust him as we should. We will complain if we have it and we will complain if we don’t. Just like miracles provided faith that Yahweh existed but that was not saving faith in the Passover, God-induced prosperity doesn’t produce saving faith either. Don’t you think that if Yahweh would just do miracles every generation we’d believe he exists, and if he would take care of all our needs then we would love and trust him? He shows us here that is not true. In fact, prosperity will be the downfall of Israel more than any other factor through the rest of this story. It is in some sense their greatest enemy.
- WHAT IS THE TEST? The most obvious test is will they do what God says or not? Will they adhere to his law or not? Will they try to stock up manna? Will they try to gather some on the Sabbath? But the commentators suggest there are two other tests that at first seem opposite each other, and yet in God’s genius, he achieved them both with the same action. First, God is testing their trust in him. Are they willing to trust God that every single day the food they need will be there? In other words, in their poverty, will they trust God every day to pull them through? But the second test is that once they do trust God to pull them through every day, they no longer need to worry about their food and their survival. They will have become affluent. And in their affluence, will they fall away from God, losing that deep feeling of need for him? Dennis Prager points out this means that God tests us both in our poverty, and in our affluence. God tests the poor, and God tests the rich. The rich think “I’m so successful, who needs God“, and the poor think there is no God. And the poor think “if I were rich, then I would believe in God“, and the rich think “if I were poor, then I would believe in God“, but they are both wrong.
  - But wait, didn’t Jesus say it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter the kingdom of heaven? Doesn’t that mean he tested the rich more? Isn’t the obvious take away from Jesus teaching that poor people go to heaven and rich people go to hell? So to get to heaven, we need to intentionally live in poverty? A lot of people think that. But if you look at what Jesus actually said, he didn’t say anything about poor people. He said it’s hard for rich people to get in heaven. And that is true, nothing draws Israel toward other gods more than affluence. The rich don’t seek God because they have what they need in this life.
  - And of course, the disciples ask Jesus, “Lord than who can be saved?” it was assumed in that day that if you were rich, it was because God blessed you. If even they can’t make it into heaven, then what hope do the rest of us have?
  - What Jesus said was completely true and appropriate in context. But the part left out of that statement is the poor don’t make it into heaven either. Who goes to heaven? Good people. You could have good poor people, and you could have good rich people. But the trouble is, you have no good people at all. Nobody is good, so nobody gets to heaven, rich or poor. So heaven is empty. The only people going there are the ones to whom the goodness of Jesus washes away their sin because they have repented of their sins and chosen to trust him. Rich and poor. Slave and free.
  - Paul addresses this in the New Testament, Philippians 4:11-13 where he attests “Not that I am speaking of being in need, for I have learned in whatever situation I am to be content. I know how to be brought low, and I know how to abound. In any and every circumstance, I have learned the secret of facing plenty and hunger, abundance and need. I can do all things through him who strengthens me.”
  - God started to reveal this truth to us, that the poor and the rich both need God, they both experience tests of faith, way back here in the desert in Exodus.

**Exodus 16:9-21 > Gathering Meat and Manna**

- MEAT AND MANNA: it doesn’t say that the meat keeps happening. It only seems to happen this time, and then we had manner every day after that. Nobody hoarded the meat because it would obviously decompose within the day or two. On this first day, everybody basically had chicken and waffles. Proof that God was from the south😃
- THE MIRACLE OF ZERO STANDARD DEVIATION: everyone eyeball how much they would need out in the field, and when they got back and measured it, everyone had exactly the same amount per person. So God could turn five jars into 5000, or 5000 into five. On the first day, one of us in this room would have tasted the sweet flavor and stocked up with as much as they could get. We are miles from anywhere, middle of the desert, God keeps leading us places where there isn’t food and water. Just like the kid who has 12 marshmallows and can’t stand to part with one for the kid who has none. God solves that little quirk of human nature.
  - PAUL QUOTES THIS: Paul quotes this verse to illustrate how we are to have Christian charity in 2 Corinthians 8:13-15. We are to provide for the needs of our brothers out of our abundance, that they may provide for our needs when the tables have turned. As he says not to just shift burden from one who has little to one who has more, but rather as a way of doing to others what you would have them do to you: if they really need help, and you can help them, then do it!
  - Not to the purely evil Marxist idea of to each according to his need, from each according to his ability. That is a satanic twisting of the idea that sounds just close enough, that people bought it and used it to torture, murder, and enslave, hundreds of millions of people in the name of self-righteous stealing from the rich to give to the poor. In this story, God decides how much everyone needed, and he supplied it exactly. In Paul’s usage, if you feel you have abundance, you should help apply the person who has a need. Seems good enough. In Marx’s usage, the state determines who has a need, the states determines who has abundance, and those who do not comply, have their families, tortured and murdered for noncompliance. To level the playing field. So the currency of the day becomes victimhood. The more victim you can claim to be, the more money you make. And the supreme crime is capability. Satan is evil, but he is not dumb.
- WHAT WOULD IT HAVE BEEN LIKE TO GATHER? Seems like it would’ve involved a lot of bending over to gather up thin flakes off the ground without getting them full of dust in the desert.
- THEN YOU SHALL KNOW THAT I AM YAHWEH YOUR ELOHIM: How many times has Yahweh said this just since the beginning of Exodus? They don’t know he is Yahweh their Elohim yet? Plagues, giant pillar of cloud and fire, splitting the Red Sea, bitter water made sweet in the middle of the desert? They don’t know yet?
  - The obvious reading of this phrase is like “After I do this, you will finally know that I am Yahweh your Elohim. What I did before was not sufficient, but now it will be.” The trouble with this statement is that it instructive, but not true. It highlights to the Hebrews just how stubborn they are, that even after all this, they still don’t know he is Yahweh their Elohim.
  - But I realized there is a second way to read this, and I think both are intended. There is a prediction: “You will know that I am Yahweh your Elohim.” And there is a time period when that will be true: “then”. It leaves out how long it will last. So it can also be read as “Only right after I do that and not long after, you will know that I am Yahweh your Elohim.” This reading has the advantage of being both true and instructive. I’ll do this miracle for you, and only “then” at that time will you know. After that, they will forget.
  - And that is indeed what they do. There was nobody in that camp by the 8th day of manna falling, rotting on all days except the Sabbath, that did not know Yahweh was taking are of them. But it doesn’t last. Just as the plagues didn’t last. The Red Sea didn’t last.
- IS YAHWEH TEACHING US TO WAIT PATIENTLY FOR MANNA? Does knowing that Yahweh is my God mean that I am supposed to just expect that he will bread down from heaven whenever I need it? Doesn’t that seem like what he is trying to teach the Hebrews? He keeps leading them places where they have to depend on him, then they grumble and don’t trust him, and then he provides for them.
  - Prior to the water, God did not promise them he would provide them water. Prior to the manner, God did not promise them that he would feed them. So what is he trying to teach us?
  - The Jews later are not supposed to wait for manna from heaven to feed them. Once they get to the promised land, God doesn’t stop it because they have no faith, he stops it because they are finally in the place where they can raise their own food once they overcome the locals. In the desert, they had no other choice. If he had not provided bread from heaven, they would have died. So does that mean that God always provides bread from heaven before anybody dies of hunger?
