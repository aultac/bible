Biblical Geography: Google Maps!

- Biblical history heavily relies on geography of Tyre, Via Maris, King’s Highway, and the fertile Nile Delta.
- BROWN VS. GREEN: Nile delta is where the food is grown.
- LAND TRANSPORTATION: Via Maris (along the sea coastal plain) and King’s Highway (east of Jordan) are the primary land routes between Egypt and Mesopotamia.
- SEA TRANSPORTATION: Tyre is the only deep water port on that side of the Mediterranean. Food is shipped from Egypt to Tyre, then distributed though Israel as the land bridge between Europe and Asia.
- SOLOMON: David consolidates power into the nation of Israel with a treaty with Hiram King of Tyre. Solomon then marries Pharaoh’s daughter, sealing control over the food production and distribution of the ancient world. And he ends up worth $2 trillion before he dies.
- CIVIL WAR: Israel splits into northern and southern after Solomon. God sends Jonah to Nineveh, capital of Assyria, and they repent so he raises them up as an empire to attack disobedient Israel.
- Mesopotamia is where most empires arise that we care about in the Biblical narrative.
- EMPIRES:
  - 722BC: Assyria invades Israel with sights set on replicating Solomon’s success, turns around at Judah because of religious revival among the Jews under Hezekiah.
  - 596 BC: Babylon (southeast of Assyria) rises to power and takes Assyria, northern Israel, and Judah and carts everybody off to Babylon.
  - Persia defeats Babylon and Cyrus creates a massive empire that loves him because he lets the people go back to their homes.
  - 322BC: Alexander the Great takes basically all of the Persian empire but from west to east, then divides among his 4 generals. The Greeks force the Hellenistic culture and the “common Greek” or “coine Greek” language on everyone. This common language provides the mechanism through which the New Testament will be written and distributed rapidly throughout this part of the world.
  - 70BC: Rome rises to power, holds Jerusalem, ready for Jesus to come around 4BC.
