In the tail between God and man, there is only one side that doesn’t know what’s going to happen and that is us. **So I think God tried all these things in order to show US that they didn’t work.**
  - DATING THE EXODUS: This is hotly debated. 1 Kings 6:1. “In the four hundred and eightieth year after the people of Israel came out of the land of Egypt, in the fourth year of Solomon's reign over Israel, in the month of Ziv, which is the second month, he began to build the house of the LORD.”
    - Solomon becomes King in 970BC
    - This is four years later: 966BC
    - 966BC + 480 years earlier: 1446BC
    - Septuagint say 440 years. Masoretic says 480 years. Reconcile by the 40 years wandering.
    - A lot of people are adamant that is it 1290BC mainly because of the cities of Pithon and Ramses built during Ramses II. I am not one of them. But they have a lot of outside-the-Bible evidence that they think points to 1290BC. Basically you have to say 480 years doesn’t mean 480 years. I don’t know the answer, but having looked at both sides, I come down on the side of 1446. **We will use the 1446 dating in this class to be consistent with the text.** Historically, archealology has much more often caught up with the Bible than the other way around.
  - EGYPT HISTORY
    - EGYPT WAS OLD: When the Israelites arrive, Egypt had been there as a powerful civilization for 2000 years. **The pyramids (\~120 of them) had been built 800 to 1000 years ago.** They had incredibly sophisticated math, chemistry, mathematics, astronomy, engineering, philosophy, and a political structure that already proved stable for two millennia. To give you an idea of how long ago this is, it would be as if the Roman empire were still here today.
    - SOPHISTICATED BUT NOT IMPERIAL: Egypt was highly sophisticated. The government traditionally was a balance of power between the Pharaoh, the priests and the military. They actually had very few corrupt evil rulers over the thousands of years of their history primarily because of this balance of power. They were not imperial: they did not venture outside of their borders very much except a few times to make preemptive attacks on the Assyrians. **They had plenty of food, that thriving civilization, a pantheon of about 80 gods with a complex well developed theology, so they just didn’t seem to feel the need to go subjugate lots of other people.**  The episode we are about to read with the Hebrews is out of character for them.

      - REMEMBER HOW WE INTERPRET: if something is unclear, the weight of evidence is with the clear things. So first ask if the unclear thing MUST be read in this way. If there is a reasonable alternative, use that. Much harder to adjust the Torah than to determine interpretation of Stephen.
        - A) STEPHEN GOT IT WRONG: under pressure, he’s reciting history from memory to save his life. This seems least likely to me, first because he was being carried by the Holy Spirit, and second because the priests don’t claim he got this wrong before killing him. If they thought he made such a dumb mistake, since their main goal was to discredit this new Christian sect, the professors would have first pointed out his foolish, elementary-school error as proof the Christians were ignorant and then killed him. But they don’t mention it at all.
        - B) SCRIBAL ERROR: original was correct, somebody copied it wrong. This should be last resort because the Bible is the most tested, studied, attacked collection of books in the history of the world, and it stands undefeated. So while we know scribal errors have occurred in copying, we also know generally when that happened and what the original said.
        - C) TELESCOPING: apparently in ancient texts it is common to conflate parts of a story for brevity. They call this telescoping. Maybe it happens, but this seems unlikely to me because it is non-falsifiable. Every time something comes up that’s wrong, we just say “telescoping”. We can’t know when it really is incorrect and when it’s just “telescoping.” That doesn’t make telescoping wrong, maybe that is what he’s doing here, but it makes it untestable.
        - D) JOSEPH’S BURIAL, ABRAHAM AS A NATIONAL NAME OR PRIOR UNRECORDED PURCHASE: Reading carefully, he refers to Joseph. We know Joseph is buried at Shechem. So this burial of “our fathers” could be Joseph and our ancestors born in Egypt, buried in Shechem. So then the only question is didn’t Jacob buy it from Hamor’s sons? It is entirely possible that Abraham also bought it in an unrecorded transaction, and Jacob bought it back after 3 generations, and that’s why it’s the only piece of ground Jacob purchases. We just don’t know about this tradition 2000 years later, but they knew about it then. This is what I think is most likely.
