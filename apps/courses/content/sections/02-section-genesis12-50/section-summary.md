

**Bucket 2: Final Summary: Genesis 12-50**

Genesis 12-50 lays the foundation of how God chose one nation to usher in the future salvation for us all. God chooses Abraham and promises to make him the father of many nations with descendants innumerable. He calls him to keep His ways of righteousness and justice and lead his family, instilling these values. We see Abraham’s concern for Lot, his prayer of intercession for the righteous in Sodom, and the love of a Father for his son Isaac. Abraham holds God and His word with the highest esteem and responds more quickly and obediently.

Isaac was the child of promise through faith in God’s word. God’s sovereignty is seen as He chose Isaac and then Jacob over Esau. Though Esau seemed the better choice by human eyes—a successful hunter— he sells his birthright for soup and marries Canaanite women. Jacob, with his mother’s help, deceives Isaac for the blessing and flees to Haran.

There Jacob serves Laban, marries Leah and Rachel, and through them has twelve sons. We see God’s faithfulness and loving kindness to Jacob even though he is quite the scoundrel, blessing him through all the twists and turns.

Joseph, Jacob’s favorite son by Rachel, is sold into slavery by his jealous brothers who return his blood-stained robe to their father who assumes he’s dead. By God’s providence, Joseph rises to power in Egypt. He interprets Pharaoh’s dreams of seven years of plenty followed by seven years of famine and is put in charge of all the food.

When famine comes, his brothers arrive from Canaan. Joseph tests them, then reveals himself, explaining it was God’s plan to preserve their lives. Pharaoh invites the family to Goshen. Jacob dies and is buried in Canaan; Joseph is embalmed and requests his bones be taken to the promised land someday.
