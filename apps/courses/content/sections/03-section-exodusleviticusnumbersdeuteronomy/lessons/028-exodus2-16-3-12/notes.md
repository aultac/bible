- GOD-FEARING: last week we had a great, engaged discussion on the idea that you are indeed supposed to fear God. May you one day have the honor of being described as a God-fearing man or woman.   **The only hope humanity has a reducing evil in this world is increasing the fear of God.**  It should be our number one goal as Christians.
- REVIEW NAMES OF GOD: Elohim (spirit beings, gods, God), Yahweh (the LORD), El Shaddai (God Almighty), the Lord (Adonai)

**Exodus 2:16-22 > Moses in Midian**

- MOSES’ CHARACTER: we see 3 actions by Moses that introduce us to his character.
  - 1: killing the Egyptian torturer,
  - 2: confronting the Hebrew abuser, and
  - 3: Saving the Midianite girls and watering their flock.
  - You could have thought with the first one that perhaps he was just angered by violence toward his own group, but now we see the kind of man that Moses is. These women have no connection to him whatsoever. You might think well they were seven pretty girls, like Jacob was suddenly the big man at the well when Rachel showed up. But we learn he didn’t even go home with them! They had to go back and find him at Jethro’s request! **He hates injustice, to the point of taking action every time. It is this man God chose to bring the law to Israel.**
- FATHER OF SEVEN DAUGHTERS: the man with seven unmarried daughters is obviously eager for the kind strong Egyptian to come back to the house.
- ZIPPORAH AND MOSES: this was not portrayed as a true love story like Rebecca and Isaac, or a tragic love story like Jacob and Rachel. She was simply given to him in marriage, she conceived, and later they have a sort of strange argument about circumcision. Clearly, this story was written by a man. Just the facts, none of the romance. Moses is portrayed as a good man who hates injustice, not a romantic or desirable man. **He doesn’t seem to turn out to be a great husband or a great father. But he is a great man.** He stood on the mountain top with Jesus when he was transfigured.
- GERSHOM: ger means “stranger” and “shom” means “there”. “Stranger there.” Stranger in a foreign land.

**Exodus 2:23-25 > God saw and God Knew**

- ANTHROPOMORPHIZING GOD: as we talked about one of our earlier classes, when we use the language of human emotions to apply to God, it **helps us relate but it sacrifices accuracy**. God remembers, but he does not forget. God knew, but he there was no time before that he didn’t know. Got heard, but it’s not like he didn’t hear their earlier cries. When the Bible says “God remembered…” it means “God has chosen to act now…”
- WHY DOES GOD CHOOSE TO INTERVENE AT SOME TIMES AND NOT OTHERS? **Why did God rescue the Jews here, but not from the Holocaust?** So many innocent people have suffered so much in this world, dying, torturous deaths and obscurity, for no good reason that we can see. The people who hurt them never paid a price.
  - THEODICY: This question is known as Theodicy. It is THE question. As the Rabbi Milton Steinberg pointed out, the religious person has to account for the existence of unjust suffering. **The atheist has to account for the existence of everything else.**
  - WHY NOT EARLIER? WHY NOT LESS PAIN AND DEATH? Dennis Prager had an interesting point here about why didn’t God intervene sooner? No matter when God would have intervened, you would’ve asked the same question: why not sooner? Why not when fewer people had died? If it had been half as many, you would’ve asked the same thing. The question is equally as valid if any evil happens at all, not just great evil. So we’re back to the same question of how can a good God exist if evil exists?
  - WESTERN MOVIES: Our Western movies have filled our heads with underdogs who win, bad guys who lose in the end, victims who get justice. To the point that we just expect it. And our movies should be like that. **Rooting for the good guy is intrinsically Christianity to its core.**  Those of you who love God must hate evil.
  - REALITY IS NOT WESTERN MOVIES: The sad truth is that In reality that rarely happens. The bad guy more often wins, the bad guy more often never pays a price, the victim does not get justice in this life. The 12-year-old young girl kidnapped by the muslim is his abused, forced wife her whole life. **How can God be good when so much evil exists, and he has the power to stop it?**
  - JOB SPLITS THE OLD TESTAMENT: The Old Testament up to Job has one core message, according to Dr. Bill Creasy: **if you do what God tells you, good will happen to you. If you don’t, it won’t.**  But then we hit Job and the story changes. The entire book is about trying to understand why bad things happen to good people. His friends tell him his suffering is proof that he’s evil. He keeps saying he knows that God is just, but can’t explain his own pain, their understanding of God is missing something. He just wants the pain to end: he even curses the day he was born. God tells him he is not in a position to understand or judge how the universe works. The rest of the Bible, to some extent, is dedicated to trying to answer Job’s question.
  - HABUKKUK: Habukkuk puts this question directly to God. **Read Habakkuk 1-2:4.**
  - MUST RELUCTANT CONVERT: From the beginning of the play “The Most Reluctant Convert”, adapted from C.S. Lewis’s book “The Problem of Pain”:
    - When I was an atheist, if you had asked me, “Why do you not believe in God?” I would have answered, “Look at the universe we live in. Mostly empty space — completely dark, unimaginably cold. Most scientists think it improbable that any planet in our solar system besides Earth sustains life. And Earth herself existed without life for millions of years and may exist for millions more when life has once again left her.
    - “And what is ‘life?’ It is so arranged that creatures live only by preying on one another. In the lower forms, this means only death. In the higher forms something called consciousness enables the living to experience pain. Creatures are born in pain, live by inflicting pain, and mostly die in pain.
    - “In the most complex of all the creatures, man, yet another quality appears: reason. And by reason we can foresee our own pain. This we call suffering. We can foresee our own death while keenly desiring to go on living. Reason enables humans to invent hundreds of ingenious ways of inflicting a great deal more pain than we could have done as irrational creatures. This power we have exploited to the full. History is our record of crime, war, disease, and terror, with just enough happiness to give us an agonized fear of losing it, and when lost, the poignant misery of remembering how good it felt.
    - “Every now and then our condition is improved by what we call civilization. But all civilizations pass away. That our own civilization will pass away is surely probable. Even if it should not pass away, we are all doomed. The universe is doomed. The astronomers hold out no hope that this planet is permanently inhabitable. The physicists hold out no hope that organic life is a permanent possibility in any part of this material universe. Not only this Earth, but the whole show; all the suns of space are to run down. Nature is a sinking ship.”
    - So! If you had asked me to believe all this that I’ve just described is the work of an omnipotent, benevolent God? I would have laughed and said the evidence points in the opposite direction. Either there is no god behind the universe, or a god indifferent to good and evil, or, worse, an evil god.
    - I never dreamed of raising the question, “If the universe is so bad, or even half so bad, how on Earth did humans ever come to attribute it to the work of a wise and good Creator? Oh, men are fools. But not so foolish as that. The direct inference from black to white, evil flower to virtuous root, senseless work to a workman infinitely wise? Staggers belief!
    - No, I did not believe God existed. And I was angry at God for not existing. Why should creatures have the burden of existence forced upon them without their consent?
  - WE JUST DON’T KNOW: In most cases, we have no idea why he intervenes or even when he is intervening. God is his own agent and often keeps his own counsel. **If he tells us why he is doing something, then we know, otherwise we are just guessing.**
  - WE KNOW WHY HERE: In this case, we do know why now and not earlier: we can see how the plan of salvation unfolded later to see why this was necessary to forge a separate people who understand what it is like to be a stranger, and he tells us that he didn’t pull them out of Egypt **until the iniquity of the Amorites was complete.** He waited until they were like the people before the flood: all their thoughts were constantly bent on evil all the time, until there was nothing good or redeemable left in that land at all.
  - WE HAVE TO TRUST GOD THROUGH STRUGGLE: But most of life we don’t really know. We just have to trust him. Because that’s what life is all about, trusting in the good God who created the universe. **Will you trust him even if the problem you have doesn’t get fixed?**
- THEIR CRIES, NOT PRAYERS, ROSE UP TO GOD: **it does NOT say they were praying to God.** It says they were crying for help and rescue, but not to God. When they get out, all they want to do is worship Egypt’s gods. There is a strong possibility that this language means after 400 years, they were crying out to the gods of Egypt, or no god at all, but God knew anyway and he came to the rescue. And this is why when Moses comes back, what he says to pharaoh is that his people need to go out into the wilderness to worship him. **They have forgotten him.** They no longer cry out to him, but he hears their cries nonetheless because he is not like the gods of Egypt, stuck in his own territory. He is the God of everywhere.

**Exodus 3:1-6 > burning bush**

- MIDIAN AND SINAI: MAP! Sinai traditional and Sinai black top
- BUSH IS SYMBOL OF THE JEWS: the burning bush has become an iconic symbol of the Jewish people. They have been burned, but never consumed, sustained by a divine flame.
- MOSES IS 80 YEARS OLD: he is near the end of his natural life if God had not extended his life another 40 years. And he’s still out tending sheep. You wonder what he thought about how his life turned out as he sat out there in the wilderness with his sheep for days on end. From my respected son in pharaoh’s court to an outcast shepherd in Saudi Arabia.
- HOLY GROUND: here we learn the holy ground is really a thing. We learned in Genesis 3 **when Cain killed his brother, but the blood soaked up the sin.** We see elsewhere that the ground itself testifies against sin that was done on it. When we get to Leviticus, we will see that the entire **purpose of all the cleanliness rituals is to cleanse an area of physical dirt on the Earth, where God can sit**. We’ve also seen now that the gods were tied to the dirt of their territories. There is something about dirt that carries spiritual significance. It is the first thing God tells Moses. He says this dirt is holy you must remove your sandals. If it means the same thing here that it does in Leviticus, then it means that **this dirt is clean from the lingering effects of sin and that is why God is there**. Symbolically, it may be that Moses had to remove his sandals because they carried with them dirt from elsewhere that he had walked, which was not holy ground.
- MOSES DOES NOT KNOW WHO THIS GOD IS: to Moses, a god meant huge statues, thousands of clean priests dressed in white, liturgy and ceremony, enormous ornate temples. Here he is talking to a bush on the backside of the desert. He has no idea who this god is.
- COMMONPLACE FOR A GOD TO SPEAK: Moses does not seem to find a God speaking to a human as unusual. Maybe the gods of Egypt did this all the time? He even feels justified in arguing with him, making excuses with him.
- NOT COMMONPLACE FOR GOD TO SAVE: what is completely unique about the God of the Torah is that he is acting here on his own. He takes the initiative. **The people did not offer sacrifices of their children so God would transactionally reciprocate with giving power to them to overthrow Egypt.** In other words, the humans had to earn the action of the gods. The Israelites didn’t even pray to God, much less do any works to earn their salvation. **God saw the injustice being done to his people and he came down to save them. No other gods were like this.**  They all demanded something. They all required human initiative. They required humans to feed them. They required elaborate ceremony. They required sacrifice. The gods were not proactive caretakers. They were not concerned with others oppressing their people.
- WHAT IF THIS HAPPENS TO YOU TODAY? If a spirit being speaks to you in a vision out of a bush that looks like it’s burning, should you assume that it is gone? How would you know that it was really one of the good guys? What does the Bible tell you to do? **The Bible tells you to test what it has to say against the Scriptures**, do not immediately assume just because it says it is speaking for God that it actually is. That is what the demon pretending to be the angel Gabriel said to Mohammed, he was a minion of Satan himself. Notice that God does not seem to be angry with Moses for questioning him, and pushing back. **In fact, it’s God himself who says here are your signs, he understands the importance of evidence when it comes to trusting him.**
- 5 REASONS WHY NOT ME: so he gives this good 5 reasons why it shouldn’t be him.

**Exodus 3:7-12 > Go back to Pharoah**

- WHO AM I? You expect God to reply with Moses‘s qualifications. This story has given us some idea of the character of Moses as one who hates evil. But he doesn’t. Instead, he just says, sort of it doesn’t matter who you are I will be with you, and that is all that matters.
- MOSES NOT SENT TO PROMISED LAND: God says he is coming down to do 2 things: take Israel out of Egypt, and bring them to land flowing with milk and honey. But he tells Moses he is only sending Moses to do the first one. Because God knows that Moses isn’t getting into the promised land.
- CHRONOLOGY PROBLEM WITH GOD’S SIGN: I think Moses would prefer the sign before he has to go see Pharoah, not after. This is a test of faith that Moses fails here, he isn’t really convinced until the staff becomes a snake in his hand, goes from healthy to leprous to healthy.
  - ONLY SEE GOD AFTERWARD: Dennis Prager points out that this may be teaching us that often we only know that God has been in our life when we look after the fact. This is exactly what Jesus told Nicodemus in John 3:8 “**The wind blows where it wishes, and you hear its sound, but you do not know where it comes from or where it goes. So it is with everyone who is born of the Spirit." We can only tell the spirit by its effects.**
  - EVERYTHING IS THAT WAY: if you think about it, in reality, the only way you are really going to 100% know that you didn’t misunderstand God, and that it will really happen, is after the fact. Prior to that, you “know” only through faith. But after that, you actually know.
  - MOSES DOES NOT KNOW THIS GOD: Don’t read our modern Christian mindset back into Moses. From Moses‘s perspective, he does not know much about this God speaking to him. He knew a lot about the Egyptian gods, and clearly the Egyptian gods had a lot of power because the Egyptian people were very prosperous. But the people of the Hebrew God were all slaves.
  - GOD IS ASKING A LOT: And this God appears to Moses, and says not “I am the creator of the entire universe.” No, he says “I am the god of your fathers, and I am stronger than Egypt and all her gods. I have a plan that I want you to carry out that sounds extremely dangerous, and will only work if I really am a higher God than the Egyptian gods.”
  - WHAT PROOF COULD GOD EVEN GIVE? I mean, think about it, just because this God can appear as a fire in a bush, or turn a staff into a snake, and can disfigure your hand, does that mean he can actually overthrow all of Egypt? Today we Christians know that he can, that he is the most high God who created and sustains everything, but Moses did not know this at that time. And what other proof could God even really give other than just to actually do it?
  - WHY JESUS WAS SO MAD AT THE UNBELIEVERS IN HIS DAY: This is why Jesus was so mad at people in his day for their unbelief. He was healing people before their very eyes and they would not take the miracles as proof that he was who he said he was. Matthew 11:20-23: “**Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! For if the mighty works done in you had been done in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented long ago in sackcloth and ashes. But I tell you, it will be more bearable on the day of judgment for Tyre and Sidon than for you. And you, Capernaum, will you be exalted to heaven? You will be brought down to Hades. For if the mighty works done in you had been done in Sodom, it would have remained until this day.**” God is asking more of Moses here than Jesus ever asked of the people of his day.
  - GOD KNOWS HE IS ASKING FOR FAITH: God is requiring faith from him and God knows this. That’s why he tells Moses that he will know for sure once it’s all done. Until then, he was going to have to trust.
- SERVE GOD SERVE PHAROAH: the Hebrew word for “serve” God here is the same verb as worship, slave, work, servant, etc. “Avad”. So in the Hebrew, there is this irony that God is sending Moses to **ask pharaoh to let his Avad go to the wild wilderness to Avad God.** Same word.
  - SLAVE TO SIN OR SLAVE TO GOD: You can either be a slave man, or a slave to God. This is the basis for Paul in Romans 6 he says that we are either slave slaves of sin leading to death or slaves of righteousness leading to life. The principal is laid down right here in the Hebrew, but the English hides it away from us.
  - PURPOSE OF FREEDOM IS SERVING GOD: And we see here that even though God identifies himself later with freedom, as “the God who took you out of Egypt,” the purpose of that freedom is to serve God. Freedom is not an end in itself: freedom has a purpose in that purpose is to be a slave to God instead of a slave to man or a slave to our own desires. **Freedom untethered from its purpose, in other words, freedom without God, does not last long and soon becomes hell on earth.**
  - FREEDOM WIHOUT GOD: The flower of freedom withers and dies without its root of serving God. Or, as founding father John Adams once wrote of the fledgling nation founded on freedom: “Our Constitution was made only for a moral and religious people. It is wholly inadequate to the government of any other”
