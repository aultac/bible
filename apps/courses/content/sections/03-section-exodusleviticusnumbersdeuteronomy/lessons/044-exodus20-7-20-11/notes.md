**Wrapping up in iniquity of fathers visited on sons**

  - CORE MESSAGE IS UNEQUAL TREATMENT: **God is saying here is that he blesses goodness far more than he punishes evil. And praise God for that.** It’s easy to get hung up on the iniquity visiting three or four generations and forget the second half of the sentence. Are you consistent? Does your revulsion at the 3 to 4 generations match your joy at the thousands of generations? Is your joy and gratitude thousands of times greater at the second half of the sentence as your revulsion is at the first?   **Isn’t the primary point of this statement that God rewards the good so much more than he punishes the bad, so repent today even if your parents were bad?** That’s what we learned in Ezekiel.
  - REMEMBER THE DESPAIR: remember the despair in reading Lamentations? How the people would have felt who thought their entire plight in torturous exile was caused by God’s punishment on them due to their father’s sins? The feeling that life is pointless. You’ve seen your sisters and mothers raped, fathers and brothers humiliated, tortured and murdered. You are never going back to the promised land again. The land flowing with milk and honey. You lost it. **Your dreams are filled with nothing but blood and pain and loss.** And there is no hope for you because of your grandparents and great-grandparents. **If only you had repented like Daniel did.**
  - REVOLUTIONARY IDEA: understand just how revolutionary this idea of repentance of the children is. It existed nowhere else in the world, and since then has not existed in any non-Judeo-Christian society. The idea that the son should not be punished for the sins of the father. It was absolutely revolutionary in human history that every person bears only their individual guilt. **If you had a bad dad you bear no guilt for that.**
  - CHILDREN OF BAD MEN: And some people really need to hear that from God. This story is adapted from an article in 1995 by Susanne Glass that appeared in the newspaper “The Independent”, which you can find here: https://www.the-independent.com/life-style/adolf-eichmann-is-a-historical-figure-to-me-ricardo-eichmann-speaks-to-suzanne-glass-about-growing-up-the-fatherless-son-of-the-nazi-war-criminal-hanged-in-israel-1595146.html

    - He finally learned the truth in his early teens. “When I was 13 or 14, I opened a magazine and saw a picture of - what do you call it in English?" He traces the shape of a noose around his neck. "Ah yes, the noose which hanged my father. Then I understood."

    - MY TAKE: **The God of Israel is the God who says the sins of the father are NOT to be visited upon the children, but the sins of the father will inevitably reverberate through 3-4 generations.**
- THOSE WHO LOVE ME AND KEEP MY COMMANDMENTS: So we as Christians have songs about God loving us. We tell people “God loves you.” We spend a lot of time on God loves you. **But do you love God? Do you want to keep his commandments because you love him?** In the same way you stay faithful to your wife because you love her? This God who is giving us these 10 Commandments rules, those who love him and keep his commandments are those he blesses. Do you spend as much time saying “I love God” as you do saying “God loves me?” **When you feel the urge to sin, to break one of his commandments, do you react with revulsion at the thought of making the one you love so sad?** Loving God is not easy, that’s why he commands it. **Life is hard, and it’s easy to blame God.** It is much easier to be angry at Yahweh than to love him. But we should love him. **And we should love him precisely for the commandments he gave us that we break.**
  - LONGEST CHAPTER IN THE BIBLE: do you know what the longest chapter in the Bible is? It is Psalm 119. 176 verses. Do you know what it is about? **It is a song about loving God’s law.**  Not even about loving God, it is specifically about loving God‘s law. As Christians we get used to seeing the law in negative terms, as judge, jury, and executioner.   **But that is wrong.**   **It is our sin that we should see in that negative light, not the law. The law is one of the best reasons to love God. Because he is good.** So when you say the words “God is good“ do you feel love toward God for the law he gave us that showed us he is good? Are you able to say as the psalmist says in Psalm 119 “**I will delight in your statutes; I will not forget your word.**” And “**Open my eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of your law.**” And “**My soul is consumed with longing for your rules at all times.” And “I find my delight in your commandments, which I love. I will lift up my hands toward your commandments, which I love, and I will meditate on your statutes**”. Those who love God want to keep his Commandments.
  - TRAFFIC LAWS: this became really clear to me when driving in a foreign country. I’m not a big fan of when a policeman turns his lights on behind me to pull me over because of our traffic laws. But having seen nations without those traffic laws, I now love the laws themselves even if I might break them. **Because without the laws, there is nothing but chaos.**
  - EASIER TO LOVE GOD IN A WORLD WHERE 10 COMMANDMENTS WERE FOLLOWED? Dennis Prager had a powerful point about this. If you imagine a world where everyone did live by the 10 Commandments, they did worship God, they honored their father and mother, they did not bear false witness, they did not steal, they did not murder or get angry with their brother, they did not commit adultery even in their heart, they did not get jealous of their neighbor’s things, **wouldn’t it be much easier to love God in such a world? If we all treated each other the way he wanted us to treat each other?** If we all actually lived by the covenant that he gave the Jews? And perhaps living in a world that does not live by those laws should cause us to love Yahweh all the more for making them. We can see what the traffic looks like in this foreign country looks like that doesn’t follow the traffic laws.
- THIS IS THE GOD WE FOLLOW: he who takes care of and rewards those who love him thousands of times more than he punishes those who hate him. Thank you Yahweh for commanding us to not make any graven images for worship.

**Exodus 20:7 > 3: name of Yahweh in vain**

- IN VAIN: what does it actually mean to use a name in vain? **What does vain mean?** If you call someone vain, it means they are obsessed with their own appearance. But that’s not what vain means here. **Here it means “without meaning” or “ without the appropriate meaning” or “ to no effect” or “without the appropriate effect”.** Like in Ecclesiastes, when Solomon says vanity of vanities all is vanity. It’s not a word we use often, so a lot of translations translate it as “meaningless, meaningless, all is meaningless”. So to take the Lord’s name in vain **is to use Yahweh‘s name to no effect, or in a meaningless way, or to an effect that is not consistent with what his name represents.**
- UNFORGIVABLE SIN: he says here that he will not forgive this sin. He will not hold him guiltless who takes his name in vain. Jesus also tells us that there is only one sin that God will not forgive: **blasphemy against the Holy Spirit**. So the two sins must be one and the same. **Whatever it means to “take the Yahweh’s name in vain” must be the same thing as blasphemy against the Holy Spiri**t, since Jesus says every sin will be forgiven the sons of man except one, and Yahweh says he will not forgive this one. **So this seems like a pretty important one to figure out.**
- RABBINIC UNDERSTANDING IS THE VAIN OATH: the Rabbis take this as meaning **you cannot swear using God’s name falsely, and also that you cannot swear to a pointless truth using God’s name**. So you cannot swear that stone is gold, but you also cannot swear that stone is stone since that is self-evident.
  - FENCE AROUND THE LAW: They extended this to avoid saying the name of God, Yahweh or Jehovah, at all. If you never say it, then you can’t accidentally swear by it incorrectly. Since this is the one thing God will not forgive, it’s not unreasonable to go out of your away to ensure it never happens. **Not 99.9999999% sure, but 100%.**
  - NEVER EVEN WRITE THE LETTERS: They extended this to include never even writing the four letters of God’s name for fear that **the Torah scroll might become worn out or accidentally thrown away which would then be throwing away the name of God.** Instead they write four underscore blanks, and they use the word Adonai (Lord) when reading Torah and Hashem (“the name”) in everyday speech.
  - YAHWEH TOLD US TO CALL HIM YAHWEH: While I admire the care and respect they give to the name of God, Yahweh specifically told Moses that his name Yahweh was to be used to identify him forever. **So in saying other things, like Hashem, or Adonai, or LORD, we are not doing what he said, which was to identify him as Yahweh.**
- OMG: so everyone’s question here is I’m sure “does this Commandment tell us that we cannot say ‘oh my God?’” **Is that the one unforgiveable sin?** I have been on both sides of this fence in my life, so I will try to do justice to each.
- DO NOT CARRY: Dennis Prager gave the most compelling answer that I have heard on this question. The Hebrew word that we translate here “take” is “nasa”. **It’s literal meaning is “to lift up”, “to raise”, or “to carry”.** It is one of the most common words in the entire Bible. It is the word used to describe what Aaron does with his breast plate containing the 12 stones that represent Israel, to pick up and carry it. It is often translated therefore as “carry”. **Ancient Hebrew had plenty of words for “speak” or “tell” or “say” but it didn’t use those words. It uses raise or carry.** And if you understand it as carry instead of take, it makes a lot more sense. Because in English, what does it even mean to take a name? It means to carry that name with you. So the command is, **thou shall not carry the name of the Lord in vain.**
  - SO WHAT IS THE INTENDED EFFECT OF YAHWEH’S NAME? When you are doing something and carrying the Lord’s name with you conspicuously, you have raised it up to carry it, it must not be meaningless or fail to achieve its effect. **So what have we learned about Yahweh so far in the Old Testament? Is the story of reality not summarized as a battle between good and evil?** If you carry the name of Yahweh, you must carry it to achieve the side of the Good. If you carry it and instead achieve evil effect, you have not only carried the name of Yahweh to no effect, but you have carried it to its opposite effect. **You have done evil in God’s name.** And “vain” instead of “evil” (ra) keeps the definition of evil in God’s. control. If he had said “evil”, then you would just explain away what you’re doing as not evil. “Vain” carries much more of a flavor of “to the expected effect” which is whatever effect God intends.
  - EVIL IN THE NAME OF GOD: And **nothing besmirches the name of God more than doing evil in his name.** Nothing keeps non-believers from the saving truth of Christ more than when people do evil in God’s name. Just as **it argues against atheism when you learn how godless Communism and Socialism committed the greatest evils mankind has ever known**, it also argues against God when his followers do evil in his name. The evil of Islam in the name of Allah argues strongly that Allah is Satan and it argues strongly that he is not to be worshipped.
  - UNFORGIVABLE TO COMMIT EVIL IN GOD’S NAME: I am convinced this is the commandment and the sin that God will not forgive: when you carry his name to do evil. The blasphemy against the Holy Spirit that Jesus said was unforgivable was when those **high priests who carried the authority of Yahweh** in their society, saw the Holy spirit performing miracles before their very eyes, and **they tell the people that the healing of this man’s hand came from Satan**, they do evil while carrying the name of God. They directly prevent people from coming to Christ to be saved after witnessing his miracles, tainting the miracle by claiming it is from Satan.
- SO IS OMG OK? When I reached that conclusion, I decided that the one unforgivable sin could not possibly be accidentally saying “oh my God” when you were stressed or amazed. Doing evil in God‘s name, that makes sense as the one unforgivable sin. But what I didn’t fully appreciate until Ray Comfort pointed out is the importance of reverence for God’s name. Just because this commandment doesn’t mean saying “oh my God“ is the one unforgivable sin, **that does not mean that it is a great idea to use our English term for God so flippantly and meaninglessly**.
  - OTHER WRONGS OUTSIDE OF 10 COMMANDMENTS: There are many wrong things that are not mentioned in the 10 Commandments. We should in fact, revere and respect God so much that we do he hesitate to use his name in meaningless context. **Or worse, to use the word God to replace a curse word.** Using the name of Jesus or the name of God to curse at something is as disrespectful as it gets. As Ray Comfort says, **would you use your mother‘s name in place of the word for excrement, to curse? If you did, wouldn’t that be dishonoring to her?** How much more so is it to the perfectly good creator of the universe?
  - WE ONLY CURSE WITH GOD’S NAME: And I find it fascinating that there is no other name with which we do this in modern society. Nearly all of our exclamations are either “oh my God,” or “Jesus Christ” or some derivative that we use to avoid saying God or Jesus: **gosh, golly, goodness, good grief, geez, gee whiz, whew lordy, lordy Moses, the list goes on….why?** **Why is it natural as humans in every language to involve some reference to God in our cursing and pointless nonsensical exclamations?** They are actually very few words you can even use. Like “wow,” “rats,” or “yikes.” **The part of us that wants to bring God into it meaninglessly is not good.** That is not the purpose of God’s name. So don’t do it. If you are going to bring God into the conversation, it should matter. But I don’t think it is the same as doing evil in God’s name.
  - RED HERRING: I consider it a red herring to make the argument that “God” is not God’s name, “Yahweh” is. We don’t even know fully how to pronounce God’s name. **The point is are you referring to the being named Yahweh or not.** And the answer is, if you talk to anyone in English, and you say “God”, you both know who you’re talking about.
  - SO JUST AVOID IT: So regardless of whether this commandment is saying that saying “OMG” is unforgivable, don’t do it. Because the reality is that it is disrespectful to Yahweh to use his name meaninglessly, or worse, in place of curse words.
- THIS IS THE GOD WE FOLLOW: who only has one unforgivable sin: doing evil in his name. Carrying his name in vain. Blasphemy against the Holy Spirit. All the others he will forgive, which is absolutely amazing to the sinner who needs forgiveness. The only hope we have of conquering evil is for Yahweh’s good name to remain unspoiled.   **The God whose name saves the world from evil and meaninglessness, that is the God we follow.**

**Exodus 20:8-11 > 4: Sabbath**

- I WILL TEACH THIS AS IT SHOULD BE TAUGHT: not necessarily what I do myself. I decided that the Sabbath is a gift, made for man and not man for the Sabbath, so I will teach the gift of the Sabbath in the hope that everyone appreciates and appropriates the gift of the Sabbath commandment in their life.
- ABRAHAM JOSHUA HESCHEL: I’ve referenced Abraham Joshua Heschel’s book, The Sabbath, in earlier classes. To help introduce the depth of the Sabbath and its implications, I would like to read some sections of his book.   **I think he properly captures the gravity of what the Sabbath is supposed to be about.**
- SEVEN GROUPS CANNOT WORK: within the commandment to not work every seventh day, there are seven groups listed that cannot work. Seven is the number of completion or completeness and it also is the number of a contract or covenant, **so this is sort of a covenant for all time with all the sevens**.
  - FOREIGNERS CANNOT WORK: if you were a foreigner, would you consider this a blessing or a religious rule imposed on you? You should see it as a blessing. For the Israelite, God left them no loopholes. **They couldn’t just force everyone else to work so they could rest on the Sabbath so they could take the day off.** The Sabbath was more than just you not working. You are not allowed to have anyone else work for you either. And between the two, you working or someone else working, it does seem worse for you to rest while you make someone else work.
  - ANIMALS CANNOT WORK: this nobody could have predicted. **God specifically says animals have to rest too**. Just as not abusing animals made it into the laws for humanity given to Noah, they make it into the Ten Commandments too. Of course, animals don’t work by themselves, so they would have still taken the human.
  - WHAT IF YOUR ANIMALS NEED WATER? There are exceptions to the work prohibition on the Sabbath, one being the preservation of life. **If your animals need food and water, you definitely give them food and water. Cows needs to be milked.** However, if you can prepare the day before to avoid having to do things on Sunday, like having a bigger water bowl, then you should do that.
- SERVANT AND SLAVE: the Hebrew word used here for male servant, and female servant is the same word for slave, “eved.” Some translations render it as slave. **So why doesn’t the 10 Commandments say you shall not have slaves?** Why does it say your slave must be allowed to rest once every seven days? We will find as we get into the rest of the Torah that what you think of as “slave” is indeed prohibited by the Torah. Servant or bondsman is more accurate to what the Torah is talking about. For example, the commandment not to steal is primarily a commandment not to steal a human, I.e. kidnapping. So slavery that involves kidnapping a person to force them to work for you is prohibited. **This commandment is not about the African slave in the 1800’s: that’s the kind of slavery that Yahweh has freed Israel from.** And even then, with the servant, what’s commanded here is not condoning slavery, it is saying you have to give everyone who works for you a break one day per week.
- ONLY COMMANDMENT WITH AN EXPLANATION: why? Dennis Prager says it could be because it is the only commandment that is a ritual, and rituals in and of themselves do not make sense without some symbolic meaning imparted to it. **So God says you rest because I rested.**
  - DEUTERONOMY 5 REASON: Moses recounts the Ten Commandments in Deuteronomy 5, and they are nearly identical except for the reason given for the Sabbath. The Sabbath is still the only one given with an explanation, which supports the “because it is the only ritual” view, but the reason is different. **There, it is because you were slaves and now you’re free.** I rested, it’s ok for you to rest, and if you don’t, you’re still basically slaves.
