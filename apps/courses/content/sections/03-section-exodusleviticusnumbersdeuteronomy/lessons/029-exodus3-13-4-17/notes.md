# 029_Exodus3:13-4:17

- POLYSEMY: “Poly” as “multiple”, “semy” like “semantic” as “meaning”. “Multiple meaning”. Polysemy can paint deep, complex pictures that monosemy can only approximate by stacking more precise words together. So when we say “this mean ___ OR ___ OR ___”, we’re stacking the terms, but it’s not quite the same picture as if the single term means all the things, giving them an intrinsic relationship. **With multi-meaning words we lose precision but we gain depth.** There are intrinsic relationships that sometimes should not be disentangled.
- WHO SPEAKS TO MOSES? Angel of Yahweh or Yahweh/Elohim?
- CONTRAST EGYPTIAN GOD’S DECADENCE AND CEREMONIES WITH SIMPLE BURNING BUSH: Moses spent 40 years watching the decadent ceremonial worship of the Egyptian gods. Processionals with thousands of perfectly dressed and pressed priests, giant ornate golden temples, music, theology, feasts. **And this God is in a tiny fire in a tiny, unremarkable bush on the backside of the desert. In the middle of nowhere. By himself. With the sheep.**
- UNIQUENESS OF THIS CONVERSATION: there is nothing else like it in any other religion in all of human history. You do not argue with gods. **If you talk back, they kill you.**  They do not give you a reasoned, patient, point by point refutation of your 5 objections. Our God wants us to struggle with him. That’s why he named his people not after a region on the earth, like Canaanite or Mexican or German or American. He named his people after himself: Israel means “struggle with God”.
- FIRST QUESTION WAS WHO AM I? And God’s answer is I am with you. His answer for why Moses is so important and capable that he can lead God‘s people out of Egypt is that God is with him. **He doesn’t mention Moses in his answer at all.**
- RESTART READING: at Exodus 3:1 to get back into the story…

**Exodus 3:13-17 > God’s name I Am**

- WHO ARE YOU? Question 1 was who am I? Question 2 is who are you? And the answer is also comprised entirely of the words “who am I” in a different order: I am who I am. Ehyeh Asher Ehyeh.
- ASKING FOR A FRIEND: Moses is kind of funny here. It’s almost as if he’s saying “So obviously I know what your name is, but, **just asking for a friend, what should I tell my friend to call you?**”
- CONSEQUENTIAL MOMENT: one of the most dramatic, consequential moments in the entire history of the universe is when Moses asked God what his name is. **All of creation is waiting breathlessly for his response.**
  - UNEXPECTED RESPONSE: And God’s response seems completely unexpected. Seems like he tells him nothing useful: what does it even mean to say I am who I am? **Imagine someone knocking on your door and you say “Who is it?” and they respond “I am who I am**.” Gee, thanks. What are we humans supposed to do with that? How does that help us relate to God or know who or what he is?
  - ANCIENT NAMES MEANT THINGS: unlike today, in ancient times a name carried the concept of essence. The name of the thing was supposed to have meaning. When Moses asked God, “What is your name?” it was a bigger question than what we mean by that today. **It was not only what do I call you, it was also what are you, what is your essence?**
  - GOD’S ANSWER IS A VERB WITHOUT TENSE: God first gives the answer of a verb: “to be.” Ehyeh Asher Ehyeh. Biblical Hebrew does not have a concept of present verb tense for “to be” like English. **There is no distinct “am, is, are” in Hebrew. Or Russian, Arabic, Chinese, or a lot of other languages.** You just say “I farmer” if you are a farmer, or “He very tall” if he is very tall. The am/is/are turns out to be **unnecessary unless you are trying to specifically say only “I am” with no predicate as in “I exist.”** To say this in Hebrew, you re-use the future tense verb as “Ehyeh.”
  - So what God said actually has four simultaneous meanings, all illuminating and accurate. Would you expect any less from the creator of the universe?
    - I AM WHO I AM
    - I WILL BE WHO I WILL BE
    - I AM WHAT I AM
    - I WILL BE WHAT I WILL BE
    - YAHWEH: tell the people that “He Is” sent you. From the root of “he is” or “he will be”
  - YOU DON’T KNOW: another way to read this is you don’t know who I am, you don’t know what I am. You can’t know who I am, and you can’t know what I am. You do not have the ability. **Imagine if he had tried to say I am three persons in one.** I am both an angel and God. I’m outside of time. I am outside of nature. So many things about God just don’t compute in our finite minds.
  - NO OTHER ANSWER IS POSSIBLE: If you think about it, it should not have surprised us. God is not “contingent” on anything else: he does not depend on anything else for his existence, otherwise what he depended on would be greater than him.   **If his name is his essence, had he followed Ehyeh with ANYTHING, as in “I am _______” his essence would have been contingent on that other thing.** For example, if he had said his essence was “I am Good”, then God’s existence requires Good to exist first, and once Goodness exists, it’s opposite also exists, Evil. So God’s essence would then depend on, or be “contingent” on evil’s existence, placing evil “above” God since God’s existence would depend on evil’s prior existence. **Not only should this answer not surprise us, it is the only answer that is even possible!**
  - GOD’S ESSENCE: So what God is revealing to Moses is that **God’s essence is existence.** God’s essence is non-contingent existence.
  - EXPLANATION FIRST BEFORE NAME: traditional view is that God gives his explanation of his name first and then gives his name (Yahweh) for all time after that. So “Ehyeh” (to be, 1st person) is both his name and the explanation (or essence) of his name Yahweh. Yahweh comes from the same root of the word “to be” and is similar to “he is.” So God‘s name, his essence really is just “being“. There’s not a lot else that man can understand about him other than that he just is. **And beyond just being, he is what he is, and he will be what he will be. He was, is, and forever will be whatever and whoever he wants to be, but, always, he is.**
  - THE FIRST COMMANDMENT: God’s name starkly highlights a very common mistake in the modern West among both believers and non-believers. And this mistake is a violation of the first Commandment coming up in Exodus chapter 20 "I am YAHWEH your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery. You shall have no other gods before me.” You have to be very careful when reading the Torah to not make up a god in your head, like “I would never believe in a God who killed the Egyptian firstborn,” and then use your made-up god to evaluate the text. **God is telling you here that you don’t know who he is in your own.** You must read this text as learning about who God is, not pre-determine who he his and then read the text. Otherwise you are holding your made-up god over the real God of the text. Put yourself in the mindset that whenever God speaks here, we learn something about him, and that is exciting. **Because he will be who he will be, so we have to keep watching to see!**
  - YOU WILL KNOW THAT I AM THE LORD: when God tells Moses that Pharoah “will know that I am Yahweh”, what he is saying then is “Pharoah will know that I am ‘He exists’”. Pharaoh will know now that there really is a Most High God, and I am him.
  - MY NAME IS IN HIM: Coming up in Exodus 23:20, Yahweh tells us his name is “in” his angel who will go before the people. “Behold, I send an angel before you to guard you on the way and to bring you to the place that I have prepared. Pay careful attention to him and obey his voice; do not rebel against him, for he will not pardon your transgression, **for my name is in him.**” My essence, the highest and only non-contingent existence, is in him. He has my power, authority, and justice. He will not pardon your rebellion.
  - JESUS EGO EIMI: **Read John 8:48-59** Greek does have both future and present first person. Ego Eimi is “I AM”. There is no more clear proof that Jesus claimed to be God than verse 58. Clearly the people there interpreted his statement as claiming to be God by using the name God gave Moses here in Exodus, that’s why they picked up stones to stone him. Notice the richness of his claim: he does not use the past tense to show he “was” when Abraham was, or the future tense “will be” . He uses the present “I AM”, not “I WAS THERE TOO.” I always am because God’s name is in me, his non-contingent existence. God’s name is in me, his essence of existence is my essence.

**Exodus 3:18-end > Go, plunder Egyptians**

- GOD SAYS PLEASE: would anyone have expected the creator of the universe to instruct Moses to say please to pharaoh?
- THE PLAGUES ARE A CHARADE: God shows that he knows what’s going to happen, but since he is just he must allow pharaoh to make the choices he is going to make so that the plagues will be justified.
- VICTIMS MUST BE POLITE: Dennis Prager points out that God commands the Israelites to be kind to their captors as they receive the gold. This is a powerful point: **being a victim does not absolve you from the requirements to love your enemies.** You’re still required to be kind.
- THE GOLD: God earned the gold for them via Joseph in the first place, so he’s having his people take it back. **And soon, the very gold that God promised them is used first to create a golden calf.** Later, more of it is used to build the tabernacle.

**Exodus 4:1-9 > staff, serpent, leprosy, blood**

- QUESTION 3: what if they don’t believe me that you talked to me? This is a very real question. If someone walked in here today and told you that God had spoken to them, would you believe them? Should you believe them? **Read Deuteronomy 13:1-5.** Deuteronomy 18:21-22 tells us also that the prophecy has to actually come true, the miracle has to actually be a miracle. **You should believe them if they agree with scripture, not leading to other Gods, and they have performed a true miracle.** The bad guys are allowed to do miracles to test our commitment to God. The Egyptian magicians turned their staffs into snakes too.
- STAFF INTO SNAKE: understand what it would mean for this to actually happen. The underlying cells trillions of trillions of proteins, all instantly converted from dead tree cells into live snake cells. **DNA rewritten, proteins deconstructed, re-sequenced, refolded, transported** where they need to go to create muscle fibers, digestion, brain, all of it. God is an awesome God.
- GRAB BY TAIL: a test of Moses’ faith. You don’t grab a scary venomous snake by the tail.
- NACHASH: the term used here for what Moses’s staff turns into is Nachash.
  - EDEN: Remember the last time we saw this term, it was the creature that was speaking to Eve in the garden of Eden.
  - BRONZE NACHASH IN THE DESERT: And the next time we will see it is when the Israelites are in the desert and they complained against God and God punished them by sending the Nachash to bite them and thousands of people died from the venom. God has Moses fashion a bronze Nachash and put it on a pole and anyone who looked on the bronze Nachash would be saved and would not die.
  - SHINING ONE: We talked at the time about how this term means more than just a snake. It literally means “shining one”. Just as the sons of God are called Morning Stars or Sons of the Dawn, like to the stars that shine in the sky, so they are likened to the shining creatures on the Earth, the glistening snake. Often other gods are represented with snake-like forms.
  - PHARAOH’S POWER SYMBOL IS A SNAKE GOD: If you think to any pictures of Pharaohs, if you can recall, what are they all wearing on their heads? A crown with a cobra on the front. **The key symbol of Pharaoh and his power was the snake, the form of the Egyptian goddess Wadjet.** The Pharaoh was not considered to be legitimate unless he was actively wearing the snake. The “Nachash”, the “Shining One.”
  - TOOL_LINK: /snakes-pharoahs/
  - YAHWEH BEATS WADJET AND PHARAOH: In this story, the Nachash from Aaron’s staff, (a “tannin”, “terrible reptile” in Pharaoh’s court) consumes the Nachash from the Egyptian priests and magicians. Their goddess Wadjet likely turned two staffs into snakes, while the snake-wearing Pharaoh watched. The “terrible reptile” of Moses consumed the snakes of the magicians. **The Nachash created by Yahweh ate the Nachash created by Wadjet.**
  - CONTROL PHARAOH’S POWER BY THE TAIL: And then, the Nachash of Yahweh was grabbed by the tail by Aaron, and turned it into a harmless wooden stick in his hand. Just feel the incredibly rich symbolism here: the Pharoah, wearing his snake crown, **in other words he sits under the snake because the snake is higher than him,** watches Moses grab the snake by the tail and render it harmless and fully controlled by Moses. In fact, it ceases to even be a Nachash, losing all power and authority in the hand of Moses. The symbol of all Pharoah’s power in Egypt was in the control of Moses’s hand.
  - TRIPLE FRACTAL:
    - Level 1 is the Nachash from the staff ultimately saves the people from the Egyptian Nachash who were keeping them in slavery.
    - Level 2 is the bronze Nachash on Moses’s staff in the desert saved the people from the other Nachash who were biting and killing them.
    - Level 3 is one day a prophet like Moses who is himself a Nachash is raised up on a pole, and all who look on him would be saved, rescued from the demon Nachash of this world who harass people into sin and so cause their destruction.
- BLOOD ON DRY GROUND: why does it specifically say the water will turn into blood on dry ground? That way no one can think that Moses was hiding some sort of “blood powder” up his sleeve.
- NILE BLEEDING: understand the powerful symbolism of this particular miracle. The Nile was everything to the Egyptians. It was a divine gift of the gods, the source of cosmic order with its regular flooding due to the god **Hapi**, the source of life, food, and their prosperity. **For its water to turn to blood on the ground before their eyes would be their own god bleeding on the ground like a corpse.** Their national identity is wrapped up in it. And Yahweh will make it bleed out on the ground through Moses.
- TWO EGYPTIAN GODS ATTACKED: the symbol of Pharoah’s power and the central artery of Egypt’s heart.

**Exodus 4:10-17 > please send someone else**

- MOSES WROTE THIS: we’re pretty sure Moses wrote this. And he included his questions and complaining. **He’s an honest man.**
- QUESTION 4 SLOW OF SPEECH: of Moses’s three actions against evil, killing the Egyptian, speaking to the Hebrew oppressing his fellow Hebrew, and standing up for Jethro’s daughters, **only the speaking one was a failure.** He may have a point.
- DOES SPEECH WIN? Turns out it is the plagues that win, not the words.
- QUESTION 5: The Truth. I just don’t want to do it. I was gung ho and all ready to do this at 40 when I killed the Egyptian. But now I’m 80. Now I’m a shepherd. This is so powerful in the story. All of us can identify with it, and none of us can understand how Moses could feel comfortable enough with God to say this directly to him. **Moses is simply intensely honest with God.**
- PUT YOURSELF IN GOD‘S SHOES: have you ever been trying to do something, and the person you’re trying to get to help you does nothing but tell you how it’s not going to work? They just keep coming up with excuses, and you keep coming up with solutions, and you get very frustrated that it feels like they aren’t trying to help at all. God is in this position now. **Moses is clearly not brainstorming and trying to work around the issues that he has identified, he’s just creating issues in the hopes that the whole thing will go away.**  There is an iconic movie line from Johnny Depp in the movie “Once Upon a Time in Mexico” that is perfect for this situation: **are you a Mexican or a Mexican’t**? It extends to our own country, are you an American or an American’t? I can just feel God’s frustration dripping out of this story.
- ANGRY WITH MOSES: it says God got angry with Moses, but **he does not appear to act on that anger**. It’s almost as if he appreciates that Moses is being honest, but the show must go on and it’s time to be done arguing.
- GET THE OLDER BROTHER: in the end, God is very smart. If I really needs to make sure that my younger son does something, I put his older brother with him. Now it will happen, Aaron will not let Moses bow out. But putting the younger brother in the position of authority over the older one in the long-term causes strife and an incident between Moses and Aaron later in the wilderness in numbers, 12.
- TAKE THIS CUP FROM ME: as we did with Joseph, we will see fractal parallels between Jesus and Moses. Here is on that is particularly stark: Jesus prays in the garden of Gethsemane, right before being arrested, beaten, and crucified, “And going a little farther he fell on his face and prayed, saying, ‘My Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me; nevertheless, not as I will, but as you will.’” Matthew 26:39. **Moses asked God here to please just take the cup from him. He didn’t want the job.**
