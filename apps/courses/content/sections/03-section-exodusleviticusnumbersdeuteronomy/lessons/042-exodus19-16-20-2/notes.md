**Exodus 19:16-end > Yahweh speaks**

- UP AND DOWN: Moses must say “are you kidding? I just got up here…”. **Trip one is Eagles wings, trip two Moses tells God the people agree, trip three is during thunder and lightning when God calls him up and then tells him to go down and bring Aaron back up.** Unclear if that’s a fourth trip. After all the rules, then he goes back up with Aaron and the elders. Moses argues with God like “I just got up here again and now you want me to go back down? **We already told them not to touch the mountain! If they do now, it’s their own fault, let them die.”**
- YAHWEH IS TERRIFYING: **the God you love is the most terrifying being that exists**. People must prepare to meet with him, and when they do, they beg to get away. **If God doesn’t scare you then you aren’t learning what the text is teaching.** The God who brought them out of Egypt on eagles’ wings is scarier than the scariest thing you have ever experienced. **The fact that this being would end up saving us by sending his own son to die in our place, to absorb his wrath in our place, is the single most unexpected feature of reality**. Nobody would have guessed it. Especially not the people quaking at the mountain.
  - PURPOSE OF SINAI: I can’t stress enough that the text told us here the purpose of the terrifying cloud and thunder was so the people would beg for a mediator. The purpose of the law, the purpose of Sinai, is that we all should be terrified of Yahweh and beg for someone to stand between us and him.
- UNIQUENESS OF COVENANT: Once again, this is completely unique in human history. Other ancient peoples had covenants that involved gods where gods witnessed them, enforced them and made bargains with kings. **What we do not have anywhere else is a god who initiates a covenant with an entire people, himself acting as their king, and giving them his moral law as the terms.** That does not happen. Contracts with gods were quid-pro-quo: you give them sacrifices so they can eat or to prove how serious you are in your request, then they give you power in return.

**READ 10 Commandments plus fear and mediator, Exodus 20:1-21**

**Intro to Ten Commandments**

- GOD SPOKE ALL THESE WORDS: the Jewish Bible scholar Benjamin Sommer points out this phrase (God speaking) occurs around 339 times in the Bible, but **there are only two places where the text does not tell us who he is speaking to.**  He just seems to be speaking.
  - CREATION OF THE WORLD IN GENESIS: The first is Genesis 1 in creation of these speaks the words that bring order to chaos, something from nothing.
  - CREATION OF MORAL ORDER IN EXODUS: The second time is here when he speaks from Mount Sinai and his rules bring order from the moral chaos of the world thus far. These 10 Commandments have done more to bring moral order in the world we live in than any other single document. T**he 10 commandments represent a new stage in creation that brings order from chaos, bringing moral order from moral chaos.**
- COUNT THE COMMANDMENTS: up to 15. WORD DOCUMENT TABLE. Jews, Protestant, and Catholics delineate them differently. **Exodus 34:28 and Deuteronomy 4:13 explicitly call these the “ten words” or “ten statements” (“decalogue”)**, but it never tells us where the delineations are, so each tradition decided for themselves.
- SECOND PERSON SINGULAR YOU: in English, we have the same word for “you“ when we’re speaking to one person or when we’re speaking to a group of people. Hebrew has a plural you and a singular you. **Yahweh is speaking to a group of people here, but all the commands are in the singular you**. In the old King James version, they use the word “thou” like **thou shall not**, and that gave you a much better clue as to what was going on because thou wasn’t usually something you would say to a group. The best we can do to approximate it here is saying to a plural group you would say “you all”. **If I stand up here and say “you all should not murder”, do you have a completely different internal feeling and if I stare right at you individually in the eye and say “you shall not murder.”**
- NEGATIVE COMMANDS: with the exception of “remember the Sabbath“ and “honor your father and mother“, the remaining eight are all negative commands. Things you are not to do. It is more important not to do bad things than it is to do good things. It’s not that you should not do good things. **But if you have to choose between not murdering, and donating to charity, pick the don’t murder. Yahweh is teaching us here that but he cares about the most in the world for humanity is that we do not sin**. All our good works are like filthy rags to him. Sin is the problem. Maybe an 80/20 mix like the 10 commandments: 80% don’t do bad, 20% actively do good.
- NO ABSTRACT PRINCIPLES: the 10 Commandments is surprisingly direct. It is specific directions for behavior much more so than platitudes or principles like **be a nice person, or do unto others what you want done to you, or don’t be selfish**. The concrete directness leaves very little room for varying interpretations.
- SPLIT BETWEEN GOD AND MAN: the first 5 Commandments all bear the name of Yahweh, and his name appears nowhere in the second five. Moses had two tablets, and since Hebrew is written right to left instead of left to right like English, the “right” tablet is the first five, and the “left” tablet is the last five.
  - MAN-GOD VS MAN-MAN: The first four Commandments (right tablet) are purely obligations between man and God. The last 5 are between man and man. The remaining commandment, #5, is honor your father and mother, and it serves as a transition between the two tablets. **This is because the reason you honor your father and mother is that they are in positions of God-like authority, and you honor the position.** God is mentioned in #5 as well, making it the link between the two.
  - TWO-PARTY CONTRACT: For the first four, they make some sense in a two party contract. One party has obligations to the other party. But stop and think for a second how unusual the second 5 Commandments are. **They don’t help Yahweh at all. God has the second party of the contract is requiring you to treat other human beings nicely. There is no benefit to him.** But he says this will be the contract. You must honor and worship me only, and what I want from you to not do these bad things to your fellow humans. **This is the God we follow.**
  - WHAT DOES GOD GET IN FIRST 5? **The other gods all required the humans to give them things in exchange for power.** In this contract between men and God, does man give God anything? **Does it ask for sacrifices? Does it ask for tithing?**   **Does it offer power?**  It says have no gods before me, make no grave images as gods for yourselves to worship, make sure you rest one day out of seven, and honor your parents. This is the contract that God signed with humanity. **There’s nothing we can do for him that he needs. This is the God we follow**.
- DID GOD GET IT RIGHT? If you were going to make the 10 commandments, would you have made them any different? Would you have left some out? Maybe add a few caveats like do not commit adultery unless your spouse is really mean to you or you and your spouse have just drifted apart? Do not lie unless the person really deserves it? Maybe you would include some more: don’t molest children, don’t be a communist? **Give this some thought now, and then ask yourself again once we’re done going through it.**
  - WHICH IS HARDEST TO KEEP: Ray Comfort had the brilliant insight to use the 10 commandments as an icebreaker when evangelizing: **which of the 10 commandments do you find hardest to keep?** Ask yourself that, and you’ll find what you need to work on. But even the one you find hardest to keep, do you think God got it wrong and should have left it out, **or is he right to put it in there and you are wrong for breaking** it?
  - RICH YOUNG RULER: Matthew 19:16-22: “And behold, a man came up to him, saying, “Teacher, what good deed must I do to have eternal life?” And he said to him, “Why do you ask me about what is good? There is only one who is good. **If you would enter life, keep the commandments.**” He said to him, “Which ones?” And Jesus said, “You shall not murder, You shall not commit adultery, You shall not steal, You shall not bear false witness, Honor your father and mother, and, You shall love your neighbor as yourself.” The young man said to him, “All these I have kept. What do I still lack?” Jesus said to him, “If you would be perfect, go, sell what you possess and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me.” When the young man heard this he went away sorrowful, for he had great possessions.“
    - HOW MANY LIES? Ray Comfort calls this approach to evangelism the “Way of the Master” because it is Jesus’s approach to the self-righteous. When someone is thinking about heaven, they generally believe what Jesus said first: follow the commandments, good people go to heaven. **And they immediately put themselves in the “I follow the commandments” category.**  But then just ask a few of the commandments: **how many lies have you told in your life?** Too many to count. What do you call someone who tells lies? A liar. **Have you ever stolen something, even if it’s small?** What do you call someone who steals? A thief. So what are you? A thief. No you’re not, you’re a lying thief. Jesus said if you look with lust on a woman you commit adultery in your heart. **Have you ever looked with lust?** Taken God’s name in vain? **If on judgment day God judges you by the 10 commandments, would you be innocent or guilty?**  Heaven or Hell?
    - NOBODY DISAGREES WITH 10 COMMANDMENTS: In all the hundreds of encounters I’ve watched with Ray doing this, you know what I have never heard? **I have never heard anyone say “lying should not be in the 10 commandments.” Or “stealing should not be in the Ten Commandments.”**  Instead you get the rich young ruler’s self-righteous response: oh yes, I keep God’s law. Conscience tells you these are the right ten, God has written that on the heart of every human since we ate the fruit in the garden of Eden.
    - WE NEED A MEDIATOR: And we knowingly broke God’s law. A**nd we have to stand before the thundering God of Sinai who will say to us in thunder and lightning like he said to Adam “Have you eaten of the tree of which I commanded you not to eat?”** And we will beg for a mediator to stand between us. **And that mediator is Jesus.**
- WHO DOES IT APPLY TO? Are these only for Israel? Are they only for children of the promise? Are they for all humanity?   **The text has God saying this to Israel,** in fact he’s going to lead with I am Yahweh your Elohim who took you out of Egypt. Seems pretty specific to Israel. People who followed their regional gods would not be addressed this way.
  - JUDAISM SAYS SEVEN NOAHIDE LAWS: Judaism says God gave the commandments to Israel only, but he gave the seven “Noahide” laws to all humanity after the flood. **They are prohibitions on idolatry, blasphemy, murder, forbidden sexual acts, stealing, eating limbs of living animals, and a command to establish courts of justice.** Trouble is, only two of those are actually in the text: capital punishment for murder and do not eat the limbs of living things. The other seven they made up, but they do align with some of the 10 commandments. **They believe their role is to convince the rest of humanity to follow those seven laws.**
  - WHAT DID GOD SAY HE CHOSE THE JEWS FOR? To be priests to the nations. There is no better way to understand Yahweh than the 10 commandments. So as priests to the nations, it is reasonable that God is giving these to the Jews so they can teach the rest of humanity that these are things God wants from all of us, not just the Jews. **Just as when you get an exam in a class you learn what the teacher thought was important, God’s law teaches us about what God finds most important.**
  - AMOS INDICTS THE NATIONS: Amos the prophet indicts Damascus, Gaza, Tyre, Edom, Moab, and Ammon largely for breaking the moral order of the Ten Commandments **as if they already know them and are bound by them.** And Israel is also indicted implicitly as they were supposed to be the priests to the nations, **Israel was supposed to carry God’s Torah to those nations and instead Israel broke the law too.**
  - PAUL GIVES US THE ANSWER: in Romans 2:12-16 he says **“For all** who have sinned without the law will also perish without the law, and all who have sinned under the law will be judged by the law. For it is not the hearers of the law who are righteous before God, but the doers of the law who will be justified. For when Gentiles, who do not have the law, by nature do what the law requires, they are a law to themselves, even though they do not have the law. **They show that the work of the law is written on their hearts**, while their conscience also bears witness, and their conflicting thoughts accuse or even excuse them on that day when, according to my gospel, God judges the secrets of men by **Christ Jesus.”** God is telling us explicitly in the 10 commandments, at least with the last 6 ethical commandments, what we all already know. **So yes, the ethical laws at least (last 6) are binding on all humanity because of the conscience.**
  - BUT AREN’T CHRISTIANS EXEMPT FROM THE LAW? This can seem a very complicated question, just read Romans and you’ll see. Then read James. But really, it’s not. **It’s a pointless question.** The God we follow, Yahweh, taught us that this is what he wants from us. We love him and don’t want to disappoint him, so whether he would hold us accountable for them or not, we should strive with everything we have to honor parents, not murder, not commit adultery, not steal, not lie, not covet because we love him and are grateful for what he’s done for us. **We love our dad, and want nothing more than to avoid doing what he hates.** So follow the 10 commandments. **Remember, they were given for our benefit anyway, not God’s.**  There is no need to even ask this question. **He will save you, you can count on that.**

**Exodus 20:1-3 > 1: no other gods**

- REMEMBER THE MORAL AMBIGUITY OF GENESIS? Remember how much we were begging for God to just come tell us what was right and wrong? To just give us the law? Well here it is. **The people and the earth itself tremble at the law, but it is finally here.**
- START WITH WHO IS THE LAW GIVER: before giving the laws, **we start with the credentials of the law giver**: Yahweh who took them out of Egypt and slavery. Remember, **the unique Elohim that defeats all the other Elohim outside his territory, who makes universal moral demands on all humanity, who works amazing miracles**. That is who is talking. **Not the creator of the universe, though**. He says I am YOUR Elohim, Israel. You listen to me.
  - IS THE FIRST SENTENCE A COMMAND? Judaism has traditionally held that one of the 10 “statements” is “I am Yahweh your Elohim who took you out of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.” Is this a command? Many people think this is a command to believe in God’s existence. But as Dennis Prager points out, **it cannot be that because by definition if Yahweh is saying all these words, he must exist to say them.**
  - COMMAND TO RECOGNIZE HIM: but there is one clear way that this is a command. You are not allowed to claim he is another God. The people standing before that mountain quaking and fear as the voice of thunder overwhelmed them we’re not wondering does God exist. But they were wondering, who is this God. And this statement leaves them no out. They cannot flame that Yahweh did not make these laws. And so in that sense, this is a command to recognize him.
  - WHY NOT SAY HE IS THE CREATOR OF THE UNIVERSE? For one, that is too abstract. He is not just the creator of the universe, like Aristotle’s “unmoved mover”, **he is identifying as the God who actually acts in the world because he hates evil and personally cares about his people**. Remember, the Egyptians had several gods who were creators of the world. It might not have been effective at all to say this because Israel would have just syncretized him with the Egyptian “ultimate” creator gods: one god among many equals.
    - COVENANT BETWEEN YAHWEH AND HIS CHOSEN PEOPLE: Since this is a covenant between Yahweh and his people, it does make some sense that he would identify as their tribal God, as opposed to the universal creator of everything. He gives them a role as priests to the rest of humanity, but the contract itself is with Israel as their Elohim. Because they’re only here because he said I will keep one nation as my inheritance, whereas the other nations were given as an inheritance to his other sons at Babel. But he said he would be his own son, and keep an inheritance for himself, that is this one nation in front of him.
  - WHY SAY HOUSE OF SLAVERY? Dennis Prager had an excellent set of points about why did Yahweh say “out of the house of slavery?” Why not just stop at “out of Egypt?”
    - QUID PRO QUO: Along the lines of this being a covenant, he opens by saying “I took you out of slavery” sort of like his part of the deal. **I did this for you, now you keep my law.** I am the God who took you out of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.
    - DO NOT ROMANTICIZE EGYPT: Another reason is though they don’t romanticize Egypt. **Which they have already started to do.** This will become the primary covenant through which they relate to God, and **they cannot relate to God without remembering that Egypt was a house of slavery.** It was not the place where they sat by the pot with meat and ate bread until they were full. **Just as we cannot relate to God unless we remember the slavery of sin we were under before being saved.** Nobody should look at sin and want to go back there. It’s not all pots of meat and your fill of bread.
    - MODERN VIEW OF EGYPT: And the third reason is so that when we read this several millennia later, we also do not romanticize Egypt.   **If it were not for this book, we would have nothing but respect for ancient Egypt.** The pyramids, the Sphinx, the longevity of their civilization, their power and wealth. But because of this book, because right here in the 10 Commandments, Yahweh says I am Yahweh your Elohim, who took you out of Egypt out of the house of slavery, **Egypt in all its splendor is actually the prototype of slavery.**
    - GOD OF FREEDOM: Finally, the way Yahweh identifies himself is first and foremost liberator. **As a God who cares about freedom.** We followed God of freedom. But he identifies as the God of freedom right before the commands to take personal responsibility. **He is saying I took you out of slavery and now you have to be responsible for your own actions**. If you are going to be free, you must have the 10 Commandments and learn to control yourself **because there is no slave master to control you now.** So I am your liberator. I am a god of freedom and freedom means personal responsibility. **You cannot survive in freedom if you lie and steal and murder. This is the God we follow.**
- THE REST IS MEANINGLESS WITHOUT THIS ONE: I think the most important reason God led with who he is in the 10 Commandments as that none of the other commandments matter that they are not from him. If Yahweh does not say murder is wrong then it is not wrong. Simply the opinion of one person that it is wrong and the opinion of another person that they can murder to get what they want. The basis of morality is that there is a moral law giver outside of creation who says murder stealing, lying, and adultery are all wrong. So we lead with that as statement number one.
