# 031_Exodus5:10-6:9

- COME AND SEE: Daniel’s point from the service last week was a great one. Another fractal between this story and Jesus’s story.
  - I WILL BE WHAT I WILL BE: God does not say to Moses, “I am outside of nature. I am the creator of the universe. I have all these attributes…,” claiming attributes for himself and then proving it by performing miracles. Instead, he says “I will be what I will be” and then he proceeds to perform a bunch of signs from which we derive his attributes, like he is outside of nature, he is not bound to his territory, he is greater than all the gods of Egypt, he does not like suffering.
  - COME AND SEE, WHO CAN DO THIS? Looking back on the story, it really is almost as if God is saying “You want to know who I am? Come and see.” And the Egyptians, the Hebrews, Moses, and us are all left asking “Who is this God who can turn the entire Nile river to blood? Who can exempt Goshen from swarms of insects? Who can summon devastating armies of locusts? Who can darken the sun? Who can kill all the firstborn of the most powerful nation on Earth?
  - JESUS SHOWN TO BE GOD: Similarly, much of the New Testament is not Jesus claiming glory for himself. We do have the instance where he does claim it, like when he said “before Abraham was, I Am”, and “You shall see the son of man coming on the clouds of glory.” We have some of his claims to be God, but what we have more of is like the entire book of Mark. It is organized such that he simply does a bunch of miracles that only God can do, and then we are left asking the question “Who is this man that even the wind and the seas obey him? Who can forgive sins but God alone?”
  - FRACTAL: A powerful fractal between Yahweh and Jesus. God tells Moses “Come and see” and Jesus says to us “Come and see.”
- LEARN TO LOVE GOD: last week I was talking with a friend about reading the Bible straight through. He was honest and said he had read it all once, but the Old Testament was confusing and he’d rather just have the New Testament. He said this half in jest, but **it got me thinking**.
  - LEARNING ABOUT GOD: we talked the last couple weeks about how God “will be who he will be”, so we have to read this book to learn about him, we can’t just reason our way to him from our own imaginations. But as we learn about him, there is a direction we should be moving in our feelings about God.
  - LOVE GOD THE FATHER: I remember the first time I went through an Old Testament commentary, one of the biggest transformations in me was the development of a deeper love of God. **I had always learned to love God only through the lens of Jesus.** I didn’t know what I was missing.
  - INCOMPLETE: But that is not the whole picture. This God who gets rejected by everyone he cares about, he is the most tragic figure in the Old Testament. **The Old Testament shows us a God that we are supposed to love even without the New Testament.** He puts up with our evil, our selfishness, our callousness toward our brothers.
  - DISCIPLINARIAN: He has to punish our injustice! As a father or mother, do you enjoy having to discipline your children? Is that your favorite part of the job? To anthropomorphize God for a minute, do you think the God of justice, goodness, and love was happy that he has to send the plagues and watch people suffer? That he was happy they had to slaughter the Canaanites? You think he wanted to send the Babylonians to murder the Jews and carry them out of their home? And why does he have to do that? Because his children rebel against him. Not once, not twice, but repeatedly through our entire lives. **Because we can’t just love our dad**.
  - APPRECIATE YAHWEH: Even if he hadn’t sent his son to die for us. He deserved our love and respect and worship and admiration. And when we read this, I hope to help impart to all of us a newfound appreciation for Yahweh. Yahweh commanded love of him to people who had no real idea Jesus would come in 1400 years. And not just any love, but love with all your heart and soul and mind.
  - YAHWEH HATES EVIL: The God who heard the pain of Israel. The God who hates evil. The God who wants heaven to be a place with people but without sin. The God who gets angry and punishes evil.
  - DO NOT FORGET THE OFFENSE DURING THE PUNISHMENT: He didn’t tell us the details of the evils done to the Hebrews, but he tells in detail the 10 punishments metered out to the Egyptians. **Do not forget the evils done to the Hebrews while watching the perpetrators suffer judgement.** Love the God who finally stepped in and did something about it because he couldn’t stand the torture any longer. **When we see the insect swarms, picture the taskmaster beating the child in front of the dad purely to watch the pain in the dad’s face.** Then imagine the insects stopping that evil while it is taking place. How can you not love THAT God?
  - PUNISHING EVIL IS WHAT WE SHOULD WANT FROM GOD: Don’t you wish God would have given Hitler a heart attack? Isn’t stoping evil exactly what we want God to do? Well he did greater things than that here. And he stopped the evil. We should love him for that. And he is about to do that in this story.

**Exodus 5:10-19 > Taskmasters and foremen: make bricks without straw**

- BRICKS STILL HAVE STRAW: they just have to go gather it themselves.
- “GATHERING” STRAW: this happens once per year at wheat harvest. You can’t just “gather” straw. If you don’t get it in the 2 to 3 weeks per year that it is being harvested, then you don’t have any straw all year long. It’s not that the Hebrews just didn’t want to work harder, it’s that it was impossible.
- HEBREW FOREMEN DON’T BLAME PHAROAH: look at the wording of their complaint. **They seem to think that the taskmasters have done this without pharaoh’s knowledge.** That’s why they met with him, thinking that if they could just tell him what the taskmasters were doing, he would fix it.
  - MISUNDERSTANDING: Clearly, this must’ve been some misunderstanding, that pharaoh ordered the same number of bricks to be made, but he didn’t know that they wouldn’t have straw. Once he understands the taskmasters took away the straw, then he would obviously reduce the quota.
  - SOVIET SHOW TRIALS: People in their position tend to hold the Ubermensch leader of their oppression in high esteem. Dennis Prager pointed out that during the great purge under Stalin in the Soviet Union, Stalin ordered all of the high ranking officials under him to be executed. It was how he intended to maintain power: no one would ever be able to get enough power to unseat him. They held show trials with the leaders where they tortured them and threatened to torture their families if they didn’t declare they had been spying for the enemy. And in these trials, the most common refrain from the accused was that they could not believe that Stalin had ordered them to be arrested. **They all said it was some kind of big mistake, and if they could just speak to Stalin,** they could clear it all up.
  - SUPREME LEADER MUST BE GOOD: They could not grasp that it was the leader they followed, the leader they respected, the leader they loved, who had ordered them disgraced, tortured and murdered. They have been trained like a dog in a shock collar that any remotely negative thought about the Great Leader could not possibly be true. As soon as the thought begins to form, the anticipation of the coming shock pushes it back down. **The poor soul is left arguing for the virtue of the very man who ordered their torture and execution**, convinced it had to all be some kind of misunderstanding.
  - The Hebrew foremen here seem to be in the same mindset: **if we could just tell pharaoh what was going on**, he would clear this all up for us.
- PHAROAH IS MACHIAVELLIAN: Pharoah was smart. **He did this specifically to undermine Moses’s and Aaron’s standing with the Hebrews.** And it works. His Machiavellian plan later would be to become the hero by graciously giving them the straw again, now Moses is effectively neutralized and the people are grateful to Pharoah.
- NICOLLO MACHIAVELLI: famous for pragmatic statecraft with his book “The Prince.” He wrote it as a gift to Lorenzo de Medici, intending it to hold all the wisdom of statecraft he had gained through his life, in the hopes it would win him favor with the Medici family and bring him out of exile. It didn’t work. But the book lives on as the basis of most modern statecraft.
  - MISREPRESENTED IN HISTORY: I was amazed when I read this book for fun years ago that 99% of the book is just intelligent, reasonable, pragmatic instructions on how to navigate difficult political situations. “Machiavellian” tends to be “maniacally evil” these days, but that is not what the book really contains.
  - FEAR AND LOVE IS BEST: What sealed Machiavelli’s fate as maniacally evil, aside from the fact that is name “Machiavelli” sounds similar to “maniacally”, is that he argued for pragmatic, outcome-based rulers where the end of “staying in power” justifies any means. “**If you cannot be both loved and feared, it is better to be feared.**” This fear is how the ultimate ruler controls his bureaucracy. But don’t miss Machiavelli’s lesser-known point: **the best ruler is the one who is both feared and loved.**
  - AVOID BEING HATED: However, Machiavelli stressed, you must avoid being “hated”. See how the foremen did not hate Pharoah? They hated Moses. They said Moses made us stink to Pharoah. **As if they deserved it.** Pharoah was feared but not hated by the foremen because it was Pharoah who gave them their positions of leadership.
  - CAMBODIA: I saw it in the documentary of the Cambodian communist torture camps titled “S21: The Khmer Rouge Killing Machine”. The filmmaker brought together survivors of the Cambodian concentration camps under Pol Pot with their captors years later. I watched with fascination because **I had no idea what the captors would say.**
  - SIGN THE FORM: In one story, they tortured a poor young woman for months, trying to get her to confess to something so they could kill her. Finally, after months of torture so horrific I will not recount it here, in a delirious stupor of unimaginable pain, they got her to sign a confession that **she had pooped in a corner. The paper went in the filing cabinet**, and the woman went to the death pits.
  - PAPERWORK IS THE PURPOSE OF THE BUREAUCRAT: Their job was simply to document that all the innocent people they were killing had confessed to crimes. Not real crimes, but they wanted to make sure there was a paper in a filing cabinet somewhere before they murdered them in cold blood by the millions. **Like they knew deep down what they were doing was wrong, and they wanted their victims to sign off on it to soothe their conscience.**
  - WHAT THE CAPTORS SAID: To a man, the captors looked haunted, like they had not slept in 20 years. These men who did the torture, who got the fake signatures on the confessions as if that made the torture and killing ok, **they all said that the same thing would’ve happened to them if they had refused.** To a man, they all saw themselves as victims.
  - MONSTERS AS VICTIMS: No human being can see himself as a monster. They could not see themselves as responsible for the evil they actually committed. They saw themselves as victims, stuck in the machine.
  - FOREMEN CHOSEN FROM HEBREWS: You see it here with the foremen of the Hebrews. Pharaoh, a master Machiavellian leader, knew this 3500 years ago. Put people from the slave population in charge with some degree of power, and make them live under the threat of torture if they do not torture their subordinates. The Nazis did the same in their concentration camps: they always put Jews in charge of other Jews, they called them “Kappos”, and they made the Kappos do most of the dirty work. **Because it is double demoralizing to be tortured by your own kind.** But the Kappos owe their elevated status to pharaoh, the man responsible for it all. So they both love and fear him.
  - PHAROAH IS PLAYING CHESS: and so **the Hebrew foremen leave not blaming Pharoah, but blaming Moses and Aaron!** He was both feared and loved. Pharoah was a chess grandmaster. An evil, Machiavellian grandmaster, but a master nonetheless. But he is about to be defeated by the ultimate grandmaster, Yahweh.
  - IS IT BAD THEN TO FEAR AND LOVE GOD? Contrast pharaoh’s fear and love with the conversation we had a couple weeks ago about how we are supposed to fear and love God. What gave Machiavelli a bad name was that he said if you cannot be both feared and loved, it is better to be feared than loved. But that does not mean that his original point of being better to be both feared and loved is not true. Pharaoh’s fear and love was pure self-aggrandizing selfish evil. **God‘s fear and love keeps you from doing evil and is directed toward the being who loved you so much that he was willing to come and be tortured in your place.** He is the only being worthy of this fear and love.

**Exodus 5:20-end > All Moses’ fault: Why God?**

- MOSES PASSES THE BUCK: Moses does to God what the foremen did to him: they don’t blame Pharoah and hate him, they blamed Moses. Moses doesn’t tell them it’s Pharoah they should hate, he turns to God and blames him. “Oh LORD, why have **YOU** done evil to this people?”
- GOD DOES NOT GET ANGRY: Moses was pretty hard on God. Directly accuses him of evil (or badness or pain, they are all the same word). But God does not get angry. He has only gotten angry with Moses once: when he said he just didn’t want to do it for no good reason. God respects when you struggle with him: his chosen people are literally called “Struggle with God”: Israel. **He gets angry when you give up.** The Bible says if you are not angry about evil, you do not love God. You’re supposed to cry out to God about evil. Why does he allow it, why can’t he prevent it? **When you quit crying out to God about evil is when you’ve given up, its when you let the evil win**. God of Israel picked a man who stands up and takes action when he sees evil. A man who is not afraid to call God himself to task for it.
- THIS SHOULD NOT BE A SURPRISE: It’s not like God didn’t warn him and tell him exactly what was going to happen. But you and I both know we would have the exact same reaction as Moses.
- WHY DIDN’T GOD JUST MAKE THE FIRST MEETING WORK? We already looked at the powerful message from God that he is outside of nature. God tells us later in this story 3 reasons that he strung it out into 10 signs:
  - Exodus 7:5 says so that the Egyptians will know that I am Yahweh.
  - Exodus 10:1-2 says “Then the LORD said to Moses, "Go in to Pharaoh, for I have hardened his heart and the heart of his servants, that I may show these signs of mine among them, and that **you may tell in the hearing of your son and of your grandson** how I have dealt harshly with the Egyptians and what signs I have done among them, **that you may know that I am Yahweh**."
  - YOU MAY KNOW I AM YAHWEH: remember Yahweh is “He Is”, as in “that you may know Ani Yahweh, I am ‘He Is’”. And what does that mean? **It means that you may know there is a “Most High Elohim”, I exist and am him, and I am in charge.**
  - Exodus 12:12 says “For I will pass through the land of Egypt that night, and I will strike all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, both man and beast; **and on all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments: I am the LORD.**”
  - NOBODY KNEW YAHWEH: Both the Egyptians and the Israelites did not know him. The 10 plagues and the extended suffering were required for them to know him. The Egyptian gods DID know him, and they intentionally oppressed his people.

**Exodus 6:1-9 > God responds, the people do not listen**

- MOSES SEEMS DISRESPECTFUL TO GOD: He accuses him directly of evil. I would have expected God to answer this way:
  - WHO DO YOU THINK YOU ARE? First off, how dare you? Who do you think you are, Moses? Where were you when I created the world? This is what he said to Job.
  - PHARAOH DID THE EVIL: Second, Pharaoh is the one who did the evil, not me! Where is your blame for Pharaoh? Did you accuse him of evil to his face? Are you more scared of him than you are of me?
  - YOU SCREWED UP: And did you tell him what I told you? Did you do what I said? No. You did not say what I told you. Did you do the miracles I told you to do? No. You did no miracles.
  - I TOLD YOU THIS WOULD HAPPEN: I already told you that Pharoah’s heart would be hardened. Why do you act now like you had no idea?
  - GOD DOES NOT ANSWER THIS WAY: But God does not answer as I would have expected him to respond. He is so much better than me. God shows a clear consideration of Moses’s feelings. He does not get angry when Moses “Isra-el”’s (struggles with God).
  - FIRST, GOD DOES NOT BLAME MOSES: he instead says “Hang on Moses, now you will see…”
  - SECOND, GOD DOES NOT ADDRESS MOSES’ ACCUSATION OF EVIL: does that mean God is the author of evil since he doesn’t refute it? No. Pharaoh is quite clearly the author of this evil, not God. God has allowed it, but he is not its author. And he understands that the mission he sent Moses on has only increased the suffering of the people at this point. So he does not respond to this charge because he understands why Moses thinks this. **God has broad shoulders, he can take your accusations.**
  - THIRD, GOD MAKES ETERNAL PROMISES: God’s promises are a way of saying **evil is temporary, but my reward is forever.** When you look back on this evil once the promises are fulfilled, it will become dimmer. The evil and pain of this life, once we make it to the afterlife, will pale in comparison to God’s goodness. **READ Romans 8:18-23.**
  - THEODICY ANSWER 3: We have seen 2 of God’s answers to evil thus far. Job: who do you think you are? Habbukuk: you focus on trusting me in the face of evil (the righteous shall live by his faith). This is the third answer is his eternal promises: the evil of this life, bad as it is, is temporary, but heaven is eternal. Anything temporary and finite is always overshadowed by anything infinite, including evil, so just hold on.
- CONTRADICTION EXPLANATION:
  - ABRAHAM ASSURANCE OF SON: after the slaughter of the Kings: Genesis 15:7: “And he said to him, "I am Yahweh who brought you out from Ur of the Chaldeans to give you this land to possess."
  - JACOB’S LADDER: Genesis 28:13: And behold, Yahweh stood above it and said, "I am Yahweh, the God of Abraham your father and the God of Isaac. The land on which you lie I will give to you and to your offspring.
  - ESSENCE VS LABEL: God clearly did say his “label” to Abraham and Jacob, so this means the “essence” side of “name”. God did not explain his name Yahweh as “I am what I am” to the patriarchs. He did not work miracles on the scale of staffs into snakes or parting red seas. Moses and Israel in this day will learn much more about God’s revealed nature than the patriarchs did. That’s what God is telling Moses here. Now you will even see Pharoah himself kick you guys out.
- CAT THAT DOESN’T WANT TO BE HELD: We saw how the Jews are symbolized by the burning bush earlier. Now, we have a new metaphor: the Jews are symbolized by a feral cat that you try to hold.
  - SCRATCHES YOU: You try to calm it down, warm it up, show it that there is some love in the world, but the cat cannot accept it. The cat can only scratch you, either in terror or retribution. It cannot receive love or comfort. We will see Israel throughout the rest of the Old Testament largely like that feral cat.
  - MIRACLES NEEDED TO BEGIN RECUPERATION: God is going to have to show these people a lot of pretty amazing signs before they are going to let him just hold them and feel peace. It is a nearly impossible task to fully recuperate someone from this kind of slavery.
  - THESE PEOPLE NEVER MAKE IT: In fact, all of them die, except for two before reaching the promised land because they just can’t mentally escape the slave master, even when physically their bodies were rescued. You can’t just come out of torture and this kind of slavery into freedom and thrive.
  - NOT EASY: That’s why his answer is to make them seven promises and does all the upcoming amazing miracles. God knows this won’t be easy.
- SEVEN PROMISES: God promises seven things to the nation of Israel. At first, these seem interesting but unremarkable. Easy to pass by in the story as just repeats of what we’ve already heard. In fact I completely passed them by for years. But it turns out they are critical.
  - “SEVEN” IS THE FIRST CLUE: Seven is the number of completion and wholeness in the Bible.
  - WRITE ON THE BOARD: 1: bring you out, 2: deliver you, 3: redeem you with an outstretched arm and with great judgments, 4: take you to be my people, 5: be your God, 6: bring you into the land, 7: give it to you for a possession.
  - To this day, the Jews drink 4 cups of wine at the Passover ritual commemorating the first four of those seven. Because seven glasses of wine would be way too much.
  - Jesus was a practicing Jew. He would have drank these 4 cups of wine at every Passover of his adult life. Except at his last Passover; the last supper. Tradition holds that the last cup he drank was the third cup because Luke says it was after dinner, which corresponds to the third cup in the ritual.
  - He drank the cup of “I will bring you out”, and “I will deliver you”, and his last cup was “I will redeem you with an outstretched arm and great judgments.” This is the cup of communion.
  - But he did not drink the fourth cup, the cup of “I will take you to be my people” until he drank it new in the new kingdom. When he took us to be his people.
  - The New Testament tells us that now, we Christians are all Jews. When you accepted Jesus as your savior, you were grafted onto the Jewish people. You became a Jew. And now let’s read those promises again, recognizing that just as the Jews today at the Passover Seder say them each year as promises to them from God, these are each also now said from God to you personally.
  - You must wait here as a slave to sin until the iniquity of the Amorites is complete. But as Paul tells us, run the race, keep the faith, and he will bring you out, he will redeem you, he will take you into a land, flowing with milk and honey, and he will be your God.
  - When you are struggling with the slavery of sin that you keep trying to stop, take heart and go back and read these promises. God will bring you out of sin. He will redeem you. He will deliver you to the land chosen for you, the new heaven and the new Earth. Guaranteed. He is “He is” (Yahweh)
- BROKEN SPIRIT: But the people don’t hear it. They don’t realize this is the creator of everything, and that he doesn’t break his promises, that he hasn’t made very many promises and they are incredibly special for him to even speak to them.
  - This is such a sad thing mentioned in such a short little phrase: “their spirit was broken.” It is possible to have a broken spirit as a broken leg. A broken leg prevents you from walking. A broken arm prevents you from throwing. A broken spirit prevents you from experiencing hope. It prevents you listening to God and seeing good and evil for what it truly is.
  - IMPOSSIBLE TO EMPATHIZE: understand that it is impossible for those of us who have never really experienced a broken spirit to really know what that is like. Just as if you’ve never broken a bone you can’t know what it is like. We should not pass over this phrase without at least acknowledging the scale of that kind of despair.
  - WE ARE CAIN: but do not read this as if the Hebrew’s reaction is not your reaction. Your spirit would be broken too. You would have reacted the same way.
  - MAN’S SEARCH FOR MEANING: A wonderful, short little book by Viktor Frankl titled Man’s Search for Meaning discusses this topic. Frankl was a Holocaust survivor. He recognized many powerful truths in that book, one of them being that the human being can survive a tremendous number of awful things: no food, torture, humiliation, etc., but they cannot survive the loss of hope. They cannot survive the truly broken spirit.
    - He saw firsthand that the prisoners who lost hope in the camp were the ones who died. They called them “Muselmanner” in camp slang. The apathetic, spiritually broken people who became like the walking dead and soon died.
    - “The prisoner who had lost faith in the future—his future—was doomed. With his loss of belief in the future, he also lost his spiritual hold; he let himself decline and became subject to mental and physical decay.”
    - “Those who know how close the connection is between the state of mind of a man—his courage and hope, or lack of them—and the state of immunity of his body will understand that the sudden loss of hope and courage can have a deadly effect.”
    - He gives an example of a spike in deaths in his camp right before Christmas 1944. Not because their physical conditions had become any worse, but because the approach of the holiday they remembered coincided with a complete loss in any hope of liberation and many prisoners “died less from lack of food or medicine than from lack of hope, lack of something to live for.”
    - But Frankl’s book has an optimistic conclusion. He realized that the Nazi’s controlled every single aspect of their lives: when they ate, when they went to the bathroom, when the slept, when they worked, when they experienced pain, when they spoke, what they spoke, everything. But even in that absolute hell, there was one thing that the Nazis could not control, no matter how hard they tried. There was one thing within Viktor that the Nazis could not take from him as their own. They could not control how he reacted to their treatment of him. And that is where he found his hope. He went on to found a famous form of psychotherapy known as logotherapy based on this idea: you control how you react to the things that happen to you. In the end, this is really the only thing you can control.
    - So when Jesus tells you to turn the other cheek when your brother slaps you, when he tells you to love you enemy when he does evil to you, he is telling you that you CAN control how you react. You are not simply a machine that is forced by your enemimes to react in pre-programmed ways.
  - HEBREW HOPES: These Hebrews had gotten their hopes up when they saw Moses and Aaron perform these signs. Their spirit got a splint to hold it back together temporarily. Their hopes weren’t up like our hopes get up, like “I hope we get ice cream after dinner tonight.” No, their hopes got up like I just saw my 10 year old daughter beaten senseless yesterday by a slave master purely to punish me for not beating my neighbor for him. Their hopes were “maybe this eternal pain is not so eternal after all. Maybe somebody does care, and maybe there really is a light at the end of this dark tunnel.”
  - BROKEN SPIRIT SPLINT: but this first encounter failed so miserably, even God himself could talk to them through Moses to give them hope, but the splint holding their broken spirit together had already come unwound. They could not hear God.
